# Shared stages, not volume, decide cyber doctrine

Doctrine in SC45 turns on what attacks share, and the evidence shows that one constant correlation between attacks measures the wrong thing. Inside one attack, targets that share the decisive access path, management plane, tool or defender succeed or fail together, at about 0.8–1.0 in every documented state case; targets reached each in its own way move almost independently, at about 0.1. Across unrelated organizations the short-run common shock is near zero (at most about 0.003 a quarter in ransomware payment data, by the notes' calculation), which supports the essay's "casino". But the doctrine test describes a campaign massed against one adapting defender, and there Russia's Shahed campaign shows a common factor 5–6 times larger than chance: 0.27–0.46 in log-odds a month (this report's conversion) against a doctrine threshold of about 0.30. No study measures how one exposed attack lowers the success of later, different attacks. The data show a fast but narrow fix for each exposed flaw, attention that saturates and fades within months, and slow structural hardening, so the model's pooled linear alertness overstates the brake on volume about tenfold at AI-era attack rates (this report's calculation). The model should build both kill chains from stages, derive its correlations from the stages that targets and attacks share, and count rare all-or-nothing failures as a mixture, because the present Gaussian formula gives a 0.54 chance to meet a plan where an exact count gives 0.90 (this report's simulation). It should also test only the shortfall against the plan, split opening salvos from month-long campaigns, let doctrine lapse, add the planners' forecast error and an assessment error that does not average out, lower the budget steepness from 8 to about 2, and narrow attribution and escalation. With these changes the fast-AI path sits at the edge of the test, a 0.796 chance at the proposed defaults and 0.77–0.83 under nearby values against the 0.80 needed (this report's calculation), so the answer rests on three values that no source measures: the chance that a campaign-wide step fails, the residual spread of a campaign against one defender, and the planners' error.

Conventions in this report: "the notes' calculation" is arithmetic by the researchers on cited inputs; "this report's calculation" is mine, from the cited inputs and the model's defaults (scripts kept outside the project). Neither is a measurement. Ranges proposed here are selected bounds that a reader can explore, not empirical intervals.

## Targets that share the decisive step fall together, at 0.8 to 1.0

Every documented multi-target state operation shows one pattern: targets that share one step succeed or fail together. In December 2015 three of three Ukrainian distribution companies lost power within 30 minutes of each other, about 225,000 customers in all, while three other intruded organizations had no operational effect ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ir-alert-h-16-056-01)). On 24 February 2022 one command through a misconfigured VPN appliance took "a majority of the previously active modems within Ukraine" off the KA-SAT network ([Viasat](https://www.viasat.com/perspectives/corporate/2022/ka-sat-network-cyber-attack-overview/)). In March 2026 one compromised administrator account wiped **about 80,000 Stryker devices through Intune in three hours**, by an anonymous source's count ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/stryker-attack-wiped-tens-of-thousands-of-devices-no-malware-needed/)). In Poland on 29 December 2025, every affected wind and solar farm had an internet-facing FortiGate VPN without multi-factor authentication. An automated plan damaged devices "in ascending order of IP addressing" and stopped at the first failure in a network segment ([CERT Polska](https://cert.pl/uploads/docs/CERT_Polska_Energy_Sector_Incident_Report_2025.pdf)).

Shared steps also fail together. One code error in the 2016 denial-of-service tool defeated the protection-relay stage at every relay it touched ([SecurityWeek on Dragos](https://www.securityweek.com/russian-hackers-behind-ukraine-power-outage-may-have-sought-more-damage)). At the Polish heat-and-power plant, an EDR canary stopped a wiper that antivirus missed, halting it "on more than 100 machines on which the file had already been executed"; a modified wiper failed the same day ([CERT Polska](https://cert.pl/uploads/docs/CERT_Polska_Energy_Sector_Incident_Report_2025.pdf)). Outcomes differed only between groups with different defenses inside one operation: the farms lost communications, the plant kept its heat supply. Mechanisms without a shared decisive step give low correlation. Worm infections inside a network had an internal correlation of 0.00–0.105 in 2004–05 honeynet data ([Böhme & Kataria](http://www.econinfosec.org/archive/weis2006/docs/16.pdf)). A ransomware attack reached on average 49% of an organization's computers, and only 4% of victims reported 91% or more ([Sophos 2024](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/the-state-of-ransomware-2024/)). The payment stage of mass zero-day extortion campaigns implies **about 0.11–0.14** (the notes' calculation from [Coveware](https://coveware.com/2026/02/why-zero-day-downstream-mass-data-extortion-campaigns-are-losing-their-bite/)).

The notes derive why the values split. With a shared step that works with chance q and a per-target step with chance r, the pairwise correlation is r(1 − q)/(1 − qr). For any chain of independent stages this becomes **ρ_w = (R − p)/(1 − p)**, where p is the chance per target and R is the product of the per-target stages only (this report's derivation, checked against an exact simulation). The default of 0.3 needs q ≈ 0.45 and r ≈ 0.44 at p = 0.2, a mixture that no case shows. Single-access operations, where the shared step is uncertain and the per-target steps are reliable, give 0.8–0.95 by the same formula at p = 0.2, above the range's upper bound of 0.8. With the stage values proposed later, ρ_w is about 0.7–0.94 today, about 0.5 for an AI campaign whose targets share one defender, and about 0.06 for AI attacks on dispersed, unrelated organizations (this report's calculation).

The one AI-orchestrated state campaign on record attacked "roughly 30 entities", each with its own reconnaissance and exploits, and "validated a handful of successful intrusions", about 10–17% if a handful means three to five ([Anthropic](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/ec212e6566a0d47/original/Disrupting-the-first-reported-AI-orchestrated-cyber-espionage-campaign.pdf)). That design should lower ρ_w, but Anthropic published no outcome per target. The count of targets needs a definition too. Capable states already hit more than three organizations or facilities in one operation without AI: three utilities and at least 27 substations in 2015, and 30 or more facilities in 2025. That contradicts the range of `targets_now` (1–3) if a target is a site, although seven of the eleven cases in the notes reached one organization (this report's count). With ρ_w derived from shared stages, a 30-site attack through one access path still gives about one independent outcome. So the model can count sites without inflating its lessons.

## Unrelated victims barely co-move; a campaign against one adapting defender moves as one

Data on many unrelated victims support near-independence in the short run. Daily arrivals of automated attacks at honeynet sensors on four continents gave a median between-firm correlation of **0.03 (0.00–0.12 by port)**, with one dominant common factor; the data are from 2004–05 and measure arrival, not success ([Böhme & Kataria](http://www.econinfosec.org/archive/weis2006/docs/16.pdf)). Coveware's share of victims who paid ran 25, 26, 26, 23, 20 and 23% from Q4 2024 to Q1 2026 ([Coveware Q4 2024](https://coveware.com/2025/02/q4-report/); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250812436558/en), search summary only; [Q3 2025](https://coveware.com/2025/10/insider-threats-loom-while-ransom-payment-rates-plummet/); [April 2026](https://coveware.com/2026/04/patch-management-goes-from-hard-to-ludicrous-in-the-agentic-ai-era/)). After a trend of −0.9 points a quarter, that leaves a short-run common shock of **at most about 0.003** (the notes' calculation, which assumes 300–1,000 cases a quarter because Coveware publishes no counts). Year-scale movement is larger because it carries drift: Sophos's encryption share fell from 76% to 70% to 50% across its 2023–2025 surveys, which implies about 0.05–0.08 ([Sophos 2023](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/press/press-releases/2023/05/data-encryption-ransomware-reaches-highest-level-in-four-years); [Sophos 2025](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/the-state-of-ransomware-2025/)).

The casino's house edge itself drifts against the attacker. The share of victims who paid fell from **85% in 2019 to about 20% in Q4 2025** ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/01/31/ransomware_payment_rates_drop/); [Coveware](https://coveware.com/2026/02/why-zero-day-downstream-mass-data-extortion-campaigns-are-losing-their-bite/)). In mass zero-day campaigns that hit many victims through one flaw, it fell from close to 25% (Accellion, 100+ victims) to close to 20% (GoAnywhere), about 2.5% (MOVEit, 2,000–3,000 organizations) and zero (Cleo, several hundred) ([Coveware](https://coveware.com/2026/02/why-zero-day-downstream-mass-data-extortion-campaigns-are-losing-their-bite/)). Coveware names backups, EDR, legal and incident-response advice, takedowns and disclosure rules: causes that the whole victim population shares. Aggregation removes short-run noise. It does not remove drift that adaptation causes.

A campaign massed against one defender behaves differently. Russia launched 8,161 Shahed-type drones in May 2026, and the monthly hit share of all launches ranged from **2.61% to 18.67%** between January 2025 and May 2026 ([Institute for Science and International Security](https://isis-online.org/uploads/isis-reports/documents/May-2026-Updated-Analysis-of-Russian-Shahed-type-UAVs-Deployment-Against-Ukraine_Final.pdf)). The month-to-month change of the log hit share had a standard deviation of 0.41 (0.24 from April 2025), against 0.066 and 0.051 for independent shots. **The common factor is 5–6 times larger than chance** (the notes' calculation; my recomputation gives 0.42 and 0.25). "Some of the largest attacks generated the lowest penetration rates": 5.27% for 892 drones on 13 May against 16.42% for 67 on 8 May. Russia's destructive cyber campaign of 2022 moved as one too: 22 organizations hit in the first week, nearly 40 destructive attacks to 8 April, about one a week to late June, and almost none in August and September ([Bateman](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2022/12/russias-wartime-cyber-operations-in-ukraine-military-impacts-influences-and-implications); [Microsoft](https://complexdiscovery.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Microsoft-Ukraine-Special-Report-April-2022.pdf)). Shared causes drove the fall: accesses "often lost as the attack was remediated" ([Google](https://services.google.com/fh/files/blogs/google_fog_of_war_research_report.pdf)), a finite stockpile of prepared attacks, and EDR detections that let customers "remediate intrusions before a destructive attack was launched" ([Microsoft](https://complexdiscovery.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Microsoft-Ukraine-Special-Report-April-2022.pdf)).

Rare common-mode events sit on top of both settings. One shared vendor dependency, the CrowdStrike outage of 19 July 2024, hit **125 Fortune 500 companies, a quarter of the list,** on one day ([Parametrix](https://parametrixinsurance.com/in-the-news/crowdstrike-to-cost-fortune-500-5-4-billion-insured-loss-range-of-540-million-to-1-08-billion)). WannaCry reached at least 81 of 236 NHS trusts through one unpatched flaw, and one kill switch stopped it ([NAO](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/investigation-wannacry-cyber-attack-and-the-nhs/)). For an attacker these jumps cut both ways: a shared flaw makes many attacks succeed together, and one shared defense makes many fail together. Reliability engineering puts the share of failures from common causes at 1–10% in practice (IEC 61508-6, seen only in a search excerpt of an [ESS safety assessment](https://indico.ess.eu/event/1235/attachments/8929/13652/ESS-0052608_-_TSS_Probabilistic_Safety_Assessment_rev1ver32.pdf)), which the notes read as a pairwise correlation of about 0.01–0.10.

The conflict between the drone data and the ransomware data resolves once the settings are named. Ukraine's air defense is one command that concentrates on "predictable routes and attack corridors"; ransomware victims are thousands of separate organizations. The Shahed figure is a planner's forecast error with drift included; the Coveware figure is a detrended residual that rests on assumed sample sizes. **A doctrine campaign masses attacks on one objective**, so its targets share one national CERT, a few EDR vendors and the attacker's own stock of tools: the Shahed and Ukraine settings apply. The essay's casino applies only to dispersed targets with no shared defender, and such targets do not serve one military objective.

### Measure the common factor in log-odds and count shared failures as a mixture

A correlation coefficient is a poor unit for this factor, because the same coefficient means a different spread at each chance of success. The standard deviation σ_C of a campaign's log-odds of success around the plan, per review, transfers across settings; it relates to the model's coefficient by ρ_b = p(1 − p)σ_C². The table converts the evidence (this report's conversion of the cited values).

| Setting | Value in the source or the notes | σ_C in log-odds | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dispersed victims, one quarter (Coveware) | ρ ≤ 0.003 at p ≈ 0.23 | ≤ 0.13 | Detrended; assumes case counts |
| Drone campaign against one defender, month to month (Shahed) | log SD 0.24–0.41, net of chance | 0.27–0.46 | Forecast error, drift included |
| Survey year to year, drift included (Sophos) | ρ 0.05–0.08 at p 0.5–0.76 | 0.45–0.66 | Upper bound |
| Current model default | ρ_b = 0.05 at p = 0.34 | 0.47 | — |
| Doctrine threshold, ±25% two-sided at 0.80 | ρ_b ≤ 0.0196 at p = 0.34 | ≤ 0.30 | σ_C ≤ 0.195/(1 − p) |
| Doctrine threshold, shortfall only at 0.80 | — | ≤ 0.45 | σ_C ≤ 0.297/(1 − p) |

The model's formula also treats every shared cause as a continuous wobble, while shared stages are all-or-nothing. If the campaign-level stages work with chance Q_C, the between-attack correlation is p(1 − Q_C)/(Q_C(1 − p)), and the present test then needs **Q_C ≥ 0.963 at any p** (this report's derivation). An exact simulation of 100 attacks on 30 targets each with Q_C = 0.9 gives ρ_b = 0.057 and a 0.90 chance to meet the plan. **The model's formula gives 0.54 for the same correlation.** For a continuous common factor of 0.3 log-odds the formula is accurate: 0.80 against 0.80 in the simulation. So the model should carry two terms. One is a continuous residual σ_C, net of the drift that modeled alertness and stock depletion already explain, so that the same co-movement is not counted twice. The other is a rare campaign-wide failure with a chance j and a depth d, counted as a mixture.

## No study measures cross-target alertness; defenders adapt at three speeds

No source measures how much one exposed state attack lowers the success of later, different attacks on unrelated targets. The indirect evidence points away from a large pooled effect. Under wartime alert in 2022, Russian intrusion attempts against 128 organizations in 42 countries outside Ukraine **succeeded 29% of the time** ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2022/06/22/defending-ukraine-early-lessons-from-the-cyber-war/)). APT12 and APT1 returned to pre-disclosure activity about 150 and 160 days after Mandiant's 2013 report ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2014/04/11/mandiant_china_apt_back_to_work/)). Volt Typhoon kept footholds "for at least five years" ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-038a)). Exploitation rose from 20% to 31% of initial access while exposures multiplied ([watchTowr on DBIR 2026](https://watchtowr.com/resources/verizon-dbir-2026-vulnerability-exploitation/)). Under the model's linear rule, 22 times today's exposed attacks would cut the log-odds by 1.1 and a 30% chance to 12.5% (the notes' calculation); no data show drops of that size.

The specific part, the fix for one exposed flaw or tool, is fast and then stalls. After Heartbleed, the vulnerable share of the Alexa top million fell from 11.5% to 3.1% in eight weeks; "patching plateaued after about two weeks", and embedded devices were 56% of the vulnerable hosts in one late-April scan ([Durumeric et al.](https://jhalderm.com/pub/papers/heartbleed-imc14.pdf)). Microsoft counted **92% of Exchange servers patched or mitigated three weeks after the ProxyLogon patches**, with about 30,000 of 400,000 still open ([The Record](https://therecord.media/microsoft-92-of-all-exchange-servers-have-been-patched-or-received-mitigations-for-the-proxylogon-bugs/)). Typical known-exploited flaws close far more slowly: half of KEV detections in 175 days and only 40% by CISA's deadline ([Bitsight](https://www.bitsight.com/sites/default/files/2024-04/bitsight-a-global-view-of-cisa-kev-catalog.pdf)), a median life of 100 days with 25% open after a year ([Cyentia](https://www.cyentia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kenna-Prioritization_to_Prediction-v3.pdf)), and 26% fully fixed in DBIR 2026 ([watchTowr](https://watchtowr.com/resources/verizon-dbir-2026-vulnerability-exploitation/)). The notes fit the share still open as r + (1 − r)e^(−kt): **k about 1.5 a year for a typical KEV and 15–33 a year for famous flaws, r about 0.05–0.3**.

The specific part is also narrow. In botnet, malware, exploit and spam feeds, fewer than 3% of indicators appeared in more than one feed, across 47 IP feeds and 8 hash feeds in 2017–18 ([Li et al.](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10094544)). At least nine of 18 in-the-wild zero-days in the first half of 2022 were variants of patched bugs, one back 12 months after its patch ([Project Zero](https://projectzero.google/2022/06/2022-0-day-in-wild-exploitationso-far.html)). Exposure also hands the flaw to others. Exploit attempts from almost 700 sources began within 24 hours of Heartbleed's disclosure ([Durumeric et al.](https://jhalderm.com/pub/papers/heartbleed-imc14.pdf)); Mandiant's average time to exploit fell from 63 days in 2018–19 to 5 days in 2023 ([Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/time-to-exploit-trends-2023)); and 28.96% of 2025's 884 new KEVs were exploited on or before their CVE date ([VulnCheck](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/state-of-exploitation-2026)). In Ukraine, Industroyer2 reused 2016 source code and was stopped before its effect; six months later the same actor used native tools and caused an outage ([ESET](https://www.welivesecurity.com/2022/04/12/industroyer2-industroyer-reloaded/); [Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/sandworm-disrupts-power-ukraine-operational-technology)). That is a strong fix against a known tool family and little defense against a new method.

The attention part saturates and fades. Organizations close **about one in ten open vulnerabilities a month whatever their size** (R² 0.93 on a log-log fit) ([Cyentia](https://www.cyentia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kenna-Prioritization_to_Prediction-v3.pdf)). When DBIR's vulnerability instances rose from 68.7 million in 2022 to 527 million in 2025, the share of KEVs fully fixed fell from 38% to 26% and the median time rose from 32 to 43 days ([watchTowr](https://watchtowr.com/resources/verizon-dbir-2026-vulnerability-exploitation/)). Notified and unnotified Heartbleed operators both converged at about 57% reacting within two weeks ([Durumeric et al.](https://jhalderm.com/pub/papers/heartbleed-imc14.pdf)). Trained phishing detection held for four months and was gone at six, an analogy for people rather than organizations ([Reinheimer et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer), abstract only). With the five-month recovery of exposed actors, attention fades at about 2–3 a year. Defenders also answer the whole threat flow: 884 new KEVs and 90 in-the-wild zero-days in 2025 ([VulnCheck](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/state-of-exploitation-2026); [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/2025-zero-day-review)), while the US was the suspected origin of 7 of 895 public operations in 2023 ([EuRepoC](https://eurepoc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/EuRepoC-Cyber-Conflict-Briefing-2023-Cyber-Activity-Balance.pdf)).

The structural part builds over years with no sign of fade. Organizations found **52% of intrusions themselves in 2025, up from 43%** ([M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)). Ukraine recorded no critical incidents in the second half of 2025 while incidents rose 37.4% to 5,927 ([UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/no-critical-cyber-incidents-recorded-in-ukraine-by-the-end-of-2025-state-special-communications-service); [dev.ua](https://dev.ua/en/news/u-2025-rotsi-kilkist-kiberatak-zrosla-na-37-1767858414)). Against that, the global median dwell time rose from 11 to 14 days ([M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)). AI speeds the vendor half of adaptation: DARPA's finalists found 86% and patched 68% of 63 synthetic flaws, in 45 minutes on average at about $152 a task ([DARPA](https://darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results)), and Anthropic's Mythos Preview found thousands of high-severity flaws in every major operating system and browser ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing)). Deployment across organizations has not sped up (DBIR 2026, above), and AI makes replacement exploits cheap: CVE-Genie reproduced 51% of 841 recent CVEs at $2.77 each ([Ullah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01835)).

These findings call for three parts in place of one pooled stock, and they weaken the brake on volume. Dilute the attention stock by the rest of the threat flow, with the force at 1–20% of it, and cap it at 0.3 log-odds. Then at **3,000–5,000 attacks a year the attention part reaches 0.06–0.15 log-odds (0.05 today), against 0.64–1.06 under the linear rule** (this report's calculation; the share and the cap are assumptions). The model's own result, doctrine near 2040 without defender adaptation, suggests that the doctrine date would then depend on the common factor and the planners' error rather than on alertness.

## The two kill chains share access and part at the payload

The standard frameworks split an effects operation where SC45 needs the split. The SANS ICS kill chain calls Stage 1 "the type of activity that would traditionally be classified as espionage", and Stage 2 adds attack development, validation on "similar or identically configured systems" and the attack itself, with "significant lag" between them ([SANS](https://icscsi.org/library/Documents/White_Papers/SANS%20-%20ICS%20Cyber%20Kill%20Chain.pdf)). MITRE ATT&CK for ICS lists 12 tactics, from initial access to impair process control and impact ([MITRE](https://attack.mitre.org/tactics/ics/)). US doctrine keeps access ("access is not guaranteed"), an intelligence gain/loss review, the effect and its assessment as separate steps ([JP 3-12](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/16681/ocr)). In the public record Stage 1 often ends without Stage 2: Havex collected for years with no Stage 2 ([SANS](https://icscsi.org/library/Documents/White_Papers/SANS%20-%20ICS%20Cyber%20Kill%20Chain.pdf)), and Volt Typhoon pre-positioned with "minimal activity" ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa24-038a)).

Stage times are the best-measured values. In six Russian operations, **first known access came about 2–11 months before the effect, a median of about 7–8 months** (the notes' calculation), which supports `lead_now` of 0.75 years. Final weaponization took 2–8 weeks: the October 2022 operational-technology (OT) component "may have been developed in as little as two months" and was ready "as early as three weeks prior" ([Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/sandworm-disrupts-power-ukraine-operational-technology)), and Industroyer2 was compiled 16 days before its run ([ESET](https://www.welivesecurity.com/2022/04/12/industroyer2-industroyer-reloaded/)). Longer chains exist: TRITON's actor was in the network "almost a year" before it reached the safety-system workstation ([Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/triton-actor-ttp-profile-custom-attack-tools-detections/)), and Stuxnet took at least five years ([Maschmeyer](https://warontherocks.com/2021/11/why-cyber-war-is-subversive-and-how-that-limits-its-strategic-value/)). Of about 12 operations that reached Stage 2, about five failed at the trigger or the effect (the notes' tally). Public evidence of a Stage 1 detection that stopped a planned effect is almost absent, partly because a stopped operation looks like espionage.

People per stage are almost unmeasured. Symantec estimated Stuxnet's core team at "five to ten people" over six months, without test, quality and management staff ([Symantec](https://www.security.com/threat-intelligence/stuxnet-dossier-espionage)); that is 2.5–5 person-years by the notes' arithmetic. The UK AI Security Institute (AISI) estimates **about 20 expert-hours for a 32-step IT network takeover range** ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities)). IT intrusion is therefore a small part of the model's 15 person-years an attack; most of it sits in target analysis, Stage 2 engineering and tests, upkeep, approvals and damage assessment. The 15 person-years joins 3,535 attack posts of 2016–2018 to 231 operations of 2011; GENIE's 1,870 staff over the same 231 operations gives 8.1 (the notes' calculation from the [leaked budget](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/natosource/us-spy-agencies-mounted-231-offensive-cyber-operations-in-2011-documents-show/)). Bought tools vary by a factor of 70: up to $5–7 million for an iOS zero-click chain and $100,000 for a router or firewall exploit ([Crowdfense](https://www.crowdfense.com/exploit-acquisition-program/)).

The gap between a technical effect and the intended effect is large. In the documented power, telecoms and industry cases, the payload produced a technical effect in about 70–90% of the cases that reached the trigger, **the purpose was met in about 15–30%, and a lasting effect in about 0–5%** (the notes' tally). Restoration took hours: "several hours" in 2015 ([E-ISAC & SANS](https://icscsi.org/library/Documents/Cyber_Events/E-ISAC%20-%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Cyber%20Attack%20on%20the%20Ukrainian%20Power%20Grid.pdf)), next day for Ukrtelecom's 85% loss of connectivity ([Bateman](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2022/12/russias-wartime-cyber-operations-in-ukraine-military-impacts-influences-and-implications)), and Iran replaced about 1,000 of 8,692 centrifuges while its enrichment output rose ([ISIS](https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/did-stuxnet-take-out-1000-centrifuges-at-the-natanz-enrichment-plant/)). At the strategic level, concessions followed 2.8% of 433 rival-state incidents in 2000–2020 ([Modern War Institute](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/tracking-competition-in-cyberspace-announcing-the-dyadic-cyber-incident-dataset-version-2-0/)), and 1,841 cyber attacks in Ukraine had "little or no impact on fighting" ([Kostyuk & Zhukov](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/zhukov/wp-content/uploads/sites/140/2017/09/2017KostyukZhukov_JCR.pdf)). These figures support `success_now` of 0.2 once "intended effect" is defined as the purpose.

AI has shortened Stage 1, not Stage 2. In GTG-1002 the AI did "approximately 80 to 90 percent of all tactical work", humans made 10–20% of the effort, and the human steps took minutes against hours of AI work: 2–10 minutes against 1–4 hours for vulnerability discovery, and 5–20 minutes against 2–6 hours for data extraction ([Anthropic](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/ec212e6566a0d47/original/Disrupting-the-first-reported-AI-orchestrated-cyber-espionage-campaign.pdf)). Anthropic's September 2026 report finds that most operations it disrupted were AI-enabled; one hacktivist campaign got into 14 of 42 targets, and another actor "never gained access" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)). In ranges without active defenders, **Mythos Preview solved the 32-step range in 3 of 10 tries in April 2026, a newer checkpoint in 6 of 10, and the 7-step OT range in 3 of 10 in May** after none in April ([AISI, April](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities); [AISI, May](https://aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing)). In the field, Microsoft finds the most complex intrusions "remain manually driven" ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/02/ai-cybersecurity-threats-microsoft-report/)), Dragos's 2026 review mentions no adversary use of AI against OT ([Dragos](https://dragos.com/resources/press-release/dragos-2026-year-in-review-new-ot-threats-ransomware)), and exploitation timing was "highly consistent" between 2024 and 2025 ([VulnCheck](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/state-of-exploitation-2026)). So `automation_now` of 0.1 is contradicted as frontier capability and fits only as average adoption in the US force, which no source measures.

The surveillance chain differs after the access stages. In 2011 **GENIE fully used only 8,448 of 68,975 implanted machines (12%) with 1,870 staff**, and a 2009 slide says "Human 'drivers' limit ability for large-scale exploitation" ([leaked budget](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/natosource/us-spy-agencies-mounted-231-offensive-cyber-operations-in-2011-documents-show/); [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2014/03/12/nsa-plans-infect-millions-computers-malware/)). Downstream, analysts were "drowning in information" ([The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2015/05/28/nsa-officials-privately-criticize-collect-it-all-surveillance/)), and one $100 million collection program produced 15 reports, two with unique information ([PCLOB](https://documents.pclob.gov/prod/Documents/OversightReport/7f5bfdcf-0ae0-4a10-b6ef-0c53137161ea/CDR%20Fact%20sheet%20FINAL.pdf)). Espionage hides longer: a median dwell of 122 days for espionage and North Korean IT-worker cases, against 14 days for all intrusions ([M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)), and public disclosure 7.7–12.8 months after access against about 2 months for disruptions ([EuRepoC](https://eurepoc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/EuRepoC-Cyber-Conflict-Briefing-2023-Cyber-Activity-Balance.pdf)). The two missions meet at the intelligence gain/loss review. Glowing Symphony drew "several 'non-concurs'" and weeks of NSC meetings ([National Security Archive](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/cyber-vault/2020-01-21/uscybercom-after-action-assessments-operation-glowing-symphony)), and the 2019 strike on an IRGC database cost "lost intelligence and lost access to a critical network" ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/cyber-command-operational-update-clarifying-june-2019-iran-operation)). No source counts how often the review delays or blocks an attack.

The surveillance stages change three parts of the model. ## Planners judge shortfall, buy assurance with redundancy and need timing

US planning standards are per-target assurance levels, not tolerances on a share of targets hit. SIOP-62 used "an assurance of 70 percent or greater" of severe damage and 95% for targets that directly threatened the United States ([CIA](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/1961-06-30a.pdf), search summary only). Its plan options put 1,706 weapons on 725 targets, 2.4 a target ([National Security Archive](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB130/press.htm)). In 1941 a heavy bomber had "a 1.2 percent probability" of hitting a 100-foot-square target, so planners sized **about 220 bombers for a 90 percent chance** ([Correll](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/PDF/MagazineArchive/Documents/2008/October%202008/1008daylight.pdf)). These levels (0.70–0.97) support the `acceptance` range of 0.7–0.95 by analogy. Planners reached them with redundancy, which guards against shortfall; over-performance never broke a plan.

The Shahed data show what a one-sided test changes. A planner who forecasts each month's hit share from the month before is within ±25% in 10 of 16 months (0.62), and in 9 of 13 from April 2025 (0.69) (the notes' calculation). **With a shortfall-only test the same campaign passes: 14 of 16 months (0.88) and 11 of 13 (0.85)** (this report's calculation from the same table). No month in 16 is within ±10%, so a `tolerance` of 0.1 is not a plausible requirement for mass effects. Front-line drones show the same pattern: 60–80% of Ukrainian FPV drones fail to reach their target, yet drones cause 60–70% of damaged and destroyed Russian systems ([RUSI](https://static.rusi.org/tactical-developments-third-year-russo-ukrainian-war-february-2205.pdf)). Commanders still prefer artillery for timed fires, because drone timing "depends on gaps in electronic protection".

Scheduled effects also need timing, which the present test omits. Close air support doctrine holds suppression of enemy air defenses to a window such as "minus 2 through minus 1 and plus 1 through plus 2" minutes around time on target, with a fallback if it is late ([JP 3-09.3](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/jp3_09_3.pdf)). Cyber doctrine asks that capabilities "reliably create the intended effects" and normally names start and stop times ([JP 3-12](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/16681/ocr)). In February 2026 the Joint Chiefs chairman called Cyber Command and Space Command "first movers" that worked "before the attack began", ahead of more than 1,000 targets struck in 24 hours ([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2026/03/cyber-space-commands-were-first-mover-strikes-iran-top-general/411819); [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/03/factbox-top-us-general-outlines-initial-timeline-us-military-operation-iran)). By the model's own definition, **plans already count on opening cyber effects in 2026**, an inference from qualitative statements with no reliability data; no public source shows doctrine for effects that must hold through a month-long campaign.

Doctrine also forms from belief and lapses after failure, which the present rule cannot show. US daylight precision bombing became doctrine in the 1930s, on a conclusion that the Air Corps reached "on the basis of very thin evidence" ([Correll](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/PDF/MagazineArchive/Documents/2008/October%202008/1008daylight.pdf)). The Butt Report of August 1941 found that only one in three attacking aircraft got within five miles ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butt_Report), search summary only), and in September Churchill approved a plan for 4,000 bombers ([Grant](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/PDF/MagazineArchive/Documents/2007/March%202007/0307gomorrah.pdf)). Excalibur shells fell from more than 50% to under 10% hits under jamming, and deliveries stopped ([Washington Post via ADN](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/05/24/russian-jamming-leaves-some-high-tech-us-weapons-ineffective-in-ukraine/)). The time to reliance was about 1–2 years in war: most Ukrainian brigades had a drone company or battalion by February 2025 ([RUSI](https://static.rusi.org/tactical-developments-third-year-russo-ukrainian-war-february-2205.pdf)), and a separate drone branch was decreed in February 2024 ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/zelensky-signs-law-on-ukraines-unmanned-systems-forces/), search summary only). In peace it took 19–31 years: guided munitions went from mass combat use in 1972–73 to 8% of munitions in 1991 and 68% in 2003 ([Correll](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0310bombs/)). So three yearly passes fit wartime intensity, while a range of 1–6 passes is short for peacetime institutions.

The planners' knowledge of the chance of success is the last gap. The model's planning variance uses the prior's 5,000 pseudo-outcomes, so the planners know p to within about 3% today (this report's calculation). Real assessments are far worse and biased upward. In Desert Storm, F-117 hits were claimed at 80% where GAO estimated a 41–60% chance per planned strike, and damage assessment covered only 357 of 862 strategic targets ([GAO](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-97-134/html/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-97-134.htm)). In Kosovo, 120 tanks claimed in June 1999 became 93 in NATO's September review and 14 in a ground survey, by a partisan source ([Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0700newsweek/)). The defense secretary was "largely disappointed" in the cyber campaign against ISIS, while Cyber Command saw "enormous strides" ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/smr/cybercon/2017/11/28/how-cybercoms-efforts-against-isis-have-changed/)). The planning variance should count the evidence, not the prior's weight, and keep a floor that lessons do not remove.

### A doctrine test built from the evidence

The proposed test keeps the model's normal approximation for the continuous part and adds the mixture, a shortfall-only window and the planners' error:

```
Π = (1 − j)·F(ε) + j·F(ε − d),          F(x) = ½·[1 + erf(x / (√2·s))]   (shortfall only)
                                        F(x) = ½·[erf(x / (√2·s)) + erf((2ε − x) / (√2·s))]   (two-sided)
s² = ((1 − p)/p)·(1 + (m − 1)·ρ_w)/(n_c·m) + (1 − p)²·σ_C² + σ_plan²
σ_plan² = σ_floor² + (1 − p)/(p·(τ + 1))
ρ_w = (R − p)/(1 − p),   R = product of the per-target stage chances
```

Here p is the chance per target without a campaign-wide failure, which sets the planned share; expected hits use p(1 − j·d). The opening test uses the same form for a salvo of about one day, an acceptance of 0.9 and an added timing chance. The table gives this report's calculation for a large campaign with d = 0.5.

| Case | p | σ_C | σ_plan | j | Shortfall test | Two-sided test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dispersed targets (ransomware-like) | 0.34 | 0.13 | 0.10 | 0.05 | 0.92 | 0.90 |
| Proposed defaults | 0.34 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.796 | 0.67 |
| Proposed, larger planners' error | 0.34 | 0.30 | 0.15 | 0.10 | 0.77 | 0.62 |
| Proposed, rarer failure | 0.34 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.05 | 0.83 | 0.70 |
| Proposed, frequent failure | 0.34 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.72 | 0.59 |
| Shahed spread, all months | 0.34 | 0.46 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.73 | 0.51 |
| Proposed defaults, higher success | 0.50 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.83 | 0.75 |

At the proposed defaults the shortfall test gives 0.796, just under the 0.80 needed, and the two-sided test fails clearly. Small changes in values that no source measures move the result across the line in both directions.

## Budgets, assessments and attribution answer weaker signals than the model assumes

Budgets follow assessed results weakly and slowly. In the best US study, **10 points on OMB's 100-point PART scale moved the President's proposed budgets by 4–5%**, and the "results" part of the score counted least ([Gilmour & Lewis](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/411/2018/07/gilmourlewis2006.pdf)). The defense budget process "typically begins more than two years before the expected year of budget execution" ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF10429.html)). The FY2026 cyber request rose 4.1% to about $15.1 billion, with about $2.6 billion for Cyber Command ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12616.html)), although US military cyber forces "have never met the department's readiness standards" ([Cobb](https://warontherocks.com/2025/05/an-insiders-guide-to-cyber-readiness/)). If 10 PART points stand for a return gap of 0.02–0.1, `steepness` would be about 0.8–4 against the default of 8 (the notes' mapping, widened here). The calibration identity makes this change large: **at a steepness of 2 the revealed value of effects falls from about 4,900 to 2,200 SEV a year, and the split's response to a return gap falls from 4.5 to 1.1 per unit** (this report's calculation). Today's split stays a rest point and becomes more stable.

The damage-assessment error is larger, shared and biased. At the defaults, the yearly assessment error is about 0.14 log units (the notes' calculation), while campaign errors in the kinetic cases ran 0.3–3 log units (the notes' conversions), with first claims the most inflated and the same error across services and contractors. A constant bias leaves the split unchanged if the revealed-preference calibration includes it, because it only rescales the calibrated value of an effect; the foresight term, which uses true value, is the one exception (this report's derivation). A bias that changes matters, for example if AI-written assessments overstate as GTG-1002's AI did, claiming "credentials that didn't work". Assessment reliability was about 0.27 in Desert Storm and 0.28 in Kosovo (the notes' calculation), which narrows `assessment_reliability` to about 0.2–0.5; the instability threshold near 0.58 lies above every analogue. Assessors without a stake in the outcome do far better: Canada's assessment unit was well calibrated over 1,514 forecasts, with an area under the curve of 0.94 ([Mandel & Barnes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4121776/)).

Learning in SC45 is slow by industrial standards, and forgetting is hard to pin down. Industry cuts cost by about 20% per doubling of output, and the Lockheed L-1011 by 30–40% ([Benkard](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7127.pdf)); at its defaults SC45 cuts attack cost by about 1.7% per doubling of today's lessons (the notes' calculation). Forgetting runs from about zero to 3.5 a year across studies: 61% of L-1011 experience survived a year (0.49 a year), early Liberty-ship estimates kept only 5–15%, and a later re-estimate found forgetting that "may well be zero" ([Benkard](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7127.pdf); [Thompson](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:53:y:2007:i:6:p:908-918), abstract only). The causes that can be counted for a cyber force sum to about 0.2–0.4 a year (the notes' calculation): tool disclosure at 0.15–0.19, converted from RAND's 6.9-year mean life of a zero-day ([RAND release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/855015)), NSA attrition of 3–6% ([Reuters via HR Reporter](https://www.hrreporter.com/news/hr-news/us-spy-agency-risks-talent-exodus-amid-morale-slump-trump-fears/286406)), and rotation of operators after 6–9 years and analysts after 4–6 years ([Cobb](https://warontherocks.com/2025/05/an-insiders-guide-to-cyber-readiness/)). Skills without practice lose half their gain in 6.5–13 months ([Tatel & Ackerman](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:40455501%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json), abstract only), which applies to the stages that only attacks practice. Steady output cannot separate learning from forgetting ([Benkard](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7127.pdf)), and the stable verdict depends on today's lessons over the prior strength (0.063 against a critical value near 0.09), which no evidence fixes to within 40% (the notes' calculation).

Attribution mixes two quantities. In 2023, **65% of 895 public cyber operations had some public attribution, but victim governments made only 12% of the attributions**, and governments attributed 3–17% of the incidents in their own country ([EuRepoC](https://eurepoc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/EuRepoC-Cyber-Conflict-Briefing-2023-Cyber-Activity-Balance.pdf)). Large destructive state attacks differ: all six reviewed were attributed, and the lag fell from 221–233 days for WannaCry and NotPetya to 54–75 days for Albania and Viasat, apart from Olympic Destroyer at 983 days ([UK FCO](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/foreign-office-minister-condemns-north-korean-actor-for-wannacry-attacks); [UK Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/russia-behind-cyber-attack-with-europe-wide-impact-an-hour-before-ukraine-invasion); [UK FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-exposes-series-of-russian-cyber-attacks-against-olympic-and-paralympic-games)). AI removes tool evidence but not attribution: CERT Polska found that an LLM-written wiper "cannot be used in attribution" and still matched the attack's infrastructure to a known cluster ([CERT Polska](https://cert.pl/uploads/docs/CERT_Polska_Energy_Sector_Incident_Report_2025.pdf)), and WithSecure made a high-confidence attribution of an AI-heavy group through its time zone, language and victims ([WithSecure](https://www.withsecure.com/en/resources-hub/w-labs/10919/)). So `attribution_now` of 0.7 holds for technical attribution of significant attacks, and the lower bound of `attribution_end` (0.05) has no support.

Formal replies are few, and escalation is rare. The EU had listed 17 individuals and 4 entities under its cyber sanctions regime by May 2025 ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/eu-extends-sanctions-legal-framework-against-cyberattacks)). The DOJ brought 14 indictments of state-backed hackers from 2014 to mid-2019, with 2 enforcement actions ([Internet Governance Project](https://www.internetgovernance.org/2019/07/30/are-indictments-in-absentia-an-effective-deterrent-for-cyber-attacks/)). In a survey of 2,993 people in three countries, escalation was the least chosen reply: 9.9% in the US, 15.8% in Russia and 5.5% in Israel ([Jensen & Valeriano](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/What_do_we_know_about_cyber_escalation_.pdf)). NATO says cumulative activity "might" amount to an armed attack, a threshold rather than a steady rise ([NATO](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/news_185000.htm)). At today's tempo of about 165 attributed attacks a year, an `escalation` of 0.1 multiplies the cost of each attack by 17.5 (the notes' arithmetic), which no evidence supports.

Every change keeps today's split as a rest point. The stage identities reproduce today's inputs, the attention part is at rest by construction, the specific part and tailoring change only along the AI path, and the structural trend and defenders' AI enter as new options of the "Response" swap group. Each new option must then pass with every run, as the contributor rules require. Lower steepness and slower adjustment keep the rest point, but the stability scans (prior strength near 3,500, forgetting near 0.2, reliability near 0.58) need a rerun. The mixture test needs an independent oracle, such as exact enumeration of Bernoulli stages, and a doctrine that can lapse is a new two-way crossing that needs a structural detection argument.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves SC45's question from "how many attacks" to "what do the attacks share, and who judges them". Volume never removes a shared cause. AI's tailoring removes the attacker's shared steps, but not the defender's: one national CERT, a few EDR vendors and one recovery practice still tie the targets of a military objective together, so the casino forms only against targets that serve no single objective. Time looks like the strongest lever in the record. The multi-target successes landed in minutes to hours (Ukraine in 30 minutes, Stryker in three hours, Poland in one morning), faster than defenders push a fix for an exposed flaw or tool, while a month-long campaign that reuses its tools invites that fix. This is an inference, because no source measures how fast detections reach a defender community. It fits a record in which US plans already count on opening effects while no public source shows doctrine for campaign effects. Under the evidence-based defender structure, alertness no longer caps volume, and the decision passes to the unmeasured common failure, the residual spread against one defender, and the planners' error.

Two results matter beyond this model. First, one correlation coefficient cannot tell a fragile campaign from a robust campaign with rare total failures: for the same coefficient, the Gaussian formula gave a 0.54 chance to meet a plan where the exact count gave 0.90, so models of campaign reliability should carry the mixture. Second, the revealed-preference calibration absorbs constant biases and the levels of value and political cost, so for the split only their changes along the AI path matter; that sharpens what to measure. The data that would settle doctrine are outcomes per target and per stage for multi-target operations, the frequency of campaign-wide failures, and the forecast errors of the planners who must count on them.
