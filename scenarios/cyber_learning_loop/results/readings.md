## Evidence

- **adoption_path** (assumption). Historical revision 8488047 uses separate saturating curves for general AI progress and slower reviewed work. The reversal term starts in year twelve.
- **defensive_capacity** (assumption). Monitoring and remediation require an illustrative tenth of analyst time at reference workload. More incoming work raises that claim, capped at one quarter. Defensive tooling and lasting improvements reduce its staffing requirement.
- **defensive_uptake** (assumption). Adoption raises the pace of discovery and patching in the historical defensive equations. The Defenders' AI option also adds up to one half to the hardening index. External uptake provides a floor.
- **dispersion_basis** (assumption). The historical common factor moves from 0.30 to 0.13 under dispersal. This aggregate calculation uses that sensitivity to reserve staff hours for correlated workload fluctuations.
- **hardening_basis** (assumption). The historical hardening and defender_ai assumptions add a yearly improvement and a gain proportional to measured uptake; both gains default to zero.
- **institutional_reliance** (assumption). At each review the institution records whether the preceding period delivered at least 120% of its opening effects-program staff-year rate. Each observation decays over a three-year time constant. A track record of 0.7 or more signals reliance. The sensitivity study varies the standard from 110% to 150%. These are illustrative service-quality thresholds.
- **learning_basis** (assumption). Retention is updated each budget period from realized work per reference employee times protected training share, with exponential decay at the historical forgetting rate. The aggregate training shares and ten percent productivity gain per unit of learned practice are illustrative.
- **mission_accounts** (identity). Reference posts are divided between surveillance and effects at each budget review. Additional funded posts belong to effects while the surge is in force.
- **national_staff** (assumption). The historical budget covers 40,535 experts, with a 3% research share and 8.7% of reference staffing attributed to effects.
- **observation** (assumption). The caller supplies aggregate incident volume and defensive tooling uptake. A volume of one is the reference workload. The two observation boundaries are replay settings.
- **output_accounts** (identity). A budget review sets the two missions’ baseline-equivalent work rates for the next period. Each accumulates that work continuously, in staff-years. Researchers, operators, collection specialists and the common analyst pool contribute their own capacities.
- **pool_accounts** (assumption). The four 2026 payroll groups reproduce the aggregate headcounts at historical revision 8488047. Nonresearch groups retain their reference proportions when the total or research share changes. At baseline the analysts work at 90% load.
- **remediation_basis** (assumption). The historical rediscovery assumption lets discovery and patching rise with uptake of defensive tools; the reference pace is one.
- **review_allocation** (assumption). The next period’s funded split adjusts one quarter of the gap between the reference effects share and its last realized share of total output. This administrative assumption balances the mix of work delivered.
- **review_work** (assumption). Illustrative office-work time savings are 35% and 45% for the two missions at full adoption. The single-share option uses slower review adoption at 20% and 30%. Scheduled reviews each take 0.02 analyst-years per analyst. Wartime review is quarterly, as at 8488047.
- **role_productivity** (assumption). The aggregate sensitivity illustration assigns up to 50% more baseline-equivalent research work on the slower adoption curve, 20% more operator support work and 30% more collection processing on the general curve. The percentages are illustrative.
- **shared_capacity** (assumption). The two missions draw on one analyst pool in proportion to their budgeted staffing. Review and training time reduce available hours. Capacity expresses a quantity of baseline-equivalent analyst work.
- **spread_basis** (assumption). To explore uncertainty about staff reserves, sample their common-factor weight uniformly from 0.05 to 0.15. The central case uses 0.10. A second experiment samples the retained-practice adjustment rate over the historical 0.1 to 0.7 per year range.
- **surge_budget** (assumption). The historical surge funds 0.25 billion 2025 US dollars per year for four years, at 0.42 million dollars per expert-year. Payroll and posts refer to equivalent full-time staff.
- **volume_account** (assumption). National annual volume is the available fraction of the smaller of demand and work capacity divided by average labor burden. The central study assumes 320 cases per year and 90% availability. Twelve baseline staff-years per case lies inside the historical 8.1 to 15.3 observed calibration range. These aggregate inputs remain fixed across adoption cases and do not infer historical case mix or success.
