# AI uplift for offensive cyber operators: share of work, speed, scale and projections

These notes collect public figures on how much of the work of offensive cyber operators AI does now, and how fast that share grows. They support the AI inputs for operators in SC45 (one country's cyber attacks and surveillance, 2026–2051). Research date: 2026-11-08.

**Scope.** The notes add new or more recent figures to the AI findings that are already in `scenarios/cyber_learning_loop/evidence/research/costs_ai.md`. Figures from that file appear only in short "context" lists, with their original sources. I did not read those sources again.

**How each finding is written.** Each finding gives the value, what it covers (who, setting, period, sample), short quotes, and the source with its date. Four labels follow:
- **Read:** "primary, full" (the fetch tool processed the whole page), "abstract", "secondary" (a report about the source), or "search summary" (I saw only a search-engine summary; treat the figure as unverified).
- **Quality:** official (a government body), reported (a vendor, platform or AI developer reports real activity or its own tests), benchmark (a lab or competition measurement), estimate (a forecast or projection), or opinion.
- **Maps to:** the SC45 input that the figure can inform. The current SC45 values are: AI share of access work 25% today and 87% at the end of AI progress; time to gain access 0.55 years today and days at the end; AI share of building and testing the effect 5% today and 57% at the end, on a slower path; AI share of running an attack 30% today and 90% at the end; supervision cost 0.2 expert-years per expert-year of AI work; one expert supervises 1 attack at once today and 10 at the end; AI multiplies each researcher's yearly finds of new flaws by 10 at the end.
- The fetch tool extracted the quotes from page text. Spot-check a quote against its page before you publish it.
- The notes give only shares, counts, rates, times and costs. They describe no method of attack.

## 1. AI use by state and criminal actors in real intrusions: share of work, speed and scale

### Takeaway
Reports from 2026 agree that AI is now in most stages of real intrusions, and one vendor (Sysdig, July 2026) documents a ransomware operation that an AI agent ran from initial access to encryption. But the newest official assessment (UK NCSC, 3 Nov 2026), Microsoft (Oct 2026) and Google (Sep 2026) say that humans still lead the most complex operations and that fully autonomous pipelines are not seen in the wild. Campaigns in 2026 reach tens of targets each, but no source gives operator headcounts, so targets per operator cannot be computed.

### Cited Findings

#### Vendor, platform and government reports, 2025–2026
- **CrowdStrike: "AI-enabled adversaries increased operations by 89% year-over-year"; the average eCrime breakout time was 29 minutes in 2025, and the fastest was 27 seconds.** Covers: CrowdStrike's tracking of "more than 280 named adversaries", calendar year 2025 against 2024. Other quotes: breakout time is "a 65% increase in speed from 2024"; adversaries "exploited legitimate GenAI tools at more than 90 organizations"; "42% of vulnerabilities were exploited before public disclosure"; "DPRK-linked incidents rose more than 130%"; "FAMOUS CHOLLIMA activity more than doubled"; "China-nexus activity increased 38% in 2025"; "Russia-nexus FANCY BEAR deployed LLM-enabled malware (LAMEHUG)"; cloud intrusions show "a 266% increase from state-nexus threat actors". — [CrowdStrike, 2026 Global Threat Report press release](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/2026-crowdstrike-global-threat-report/), 24 Feb 2026. Read: primary, full (the press release; I did not read the full report). Quality: reported (vendor telemetry). The release does not define an "AI-enabled adversary" or say how it counts operations. Maps to: the growth rate of AI use by real actors (adoption path). Breakout time sets the speed of movement inside a network after access for all attackers, not only AI users. The SC45 notes cited the breakout values from a search summary; this read confirms them.
- **JADEPUFFER: a vendor documents a ransomware operation that an AI agent ran from initial access to encryption.** Covers: one victim environment (an internet-facing AI workflow server and a production database server), reported by Sysdig's Threat Research Team. Quotes: "600+ distinct, purposeful payloads executed in a compressed window"; a corrective payload followed a failed login "31 seconds later"; the agent encrypted 1,342 configuration items and dropped the original tables; Sysdig "assess[es]" this as "the first documented case of agentic ransomware" and says "the evidence points to an autonomous agent". The agent did reconnaissance, credential theft, movement to a second server, persistence and the destructive step. Sysdig does not name the model, give the total elapsed time, or describe a human role. It cannot tell whether a human set the payment address. Its comparison with humans ("takes much longer than 31 seconds") is not measured. — [Sysdig, JADEPUFFER: agentic ransomware for automated database extortion](https://www.sysdig.com/blog/jadepuffer-agentic-ransomware-for-automated-database-extortion), 1 Jul 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: reported (vendor, one case; the attack used known flaws and weak settings). The Cloud Security Alliance summary says "a single autonomous agent executed the entire sequence without a human directing each step" ([CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-jadepuffer-agentic-ransomware-20260706-csa/), 6 Jul 2026; Read: secondary, full). Conflict: a search summary of secondary coverage says that a human chose the target and negotiated the ransom; I could not trace this to a named source, and Sysdig does not say it. The Decoder reports no independent confirmation from the victim, the police or other firms ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/jadepuffer-is-the-first-agentic-ransomware-operation-and-it-exposes-old-security-sins-at-machine-speed/); search summary only). Maps to: AI share of running an attack and of the effect step for IT targets (near 100% in one criminal case); supervision span (no per-step direction).
- **UK NCSC, 3 Nov 2026: "It is almost certain AI will act as a force multiplier across the cyber threat landscape"; humans still lead operations.** Covers: the NCSC assessment for 1 Sep 2025 to 31 Aug 2026. Quotes: "Loss of control whilst running an agentic AI will highly likely hinder threat actors"; "China highly likely has the intent and is best positioned to use AI for operations against critical national infrastructure"; "it is highly likely defenders and maintainers of systems will face a race to shorten patch deployment timelines"; "It is highly likely AI malware will be used in a ransomware attack on UK CNI". The page judges it highly likely that AI raises speed, scale and efficiency but does not conduct end-to-end attacks by itself, and that human operators stay essential (fetch-tool paraphrase). The page gives no percentages. — [NCSC Annual Review 2026, AI impact on the cyber threat](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/annual-review-2026/understanding-threat/ai-impact-cyber-threat), 3 Nov 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: official. Maps to: AI share of running an attack today (below 100%); which states adopt fastest (China named).
- **Google, Q3 2026: an "autonomous, multi-agent attack framework" took thousands of credentials within six hours; Google has seen no fully autonomous, end-to-end pipeline that exploits unknown flaws in the wild.** Covers: a financially motivated actor tracked as TeamPCP (also Altered Spider and UNC6780), against cloud infrastructure. — [TechNode Global on the Google Threat Intelligence Group tracker](https://technode.global/2026/09/09/google-threat-actors-agentic-ai-cyberattacks/), 9 Sep 2026. Read: search summary only. Quality: reported (vendor). The SC45 notes already cite this tracker through Help Net Security ("less than six hours"). Reports differ on whether Mandiant or the Google group saw the case. Maps to: time to gain access to soft cloud targets (hours); span (one actor, thousands of accounts).
- **HackerOne, 2025: "Autonomous agents submitted 560+ valid reports"; "70% of surveyed researchers now use AI tools in their workflow".** Covers: the HackerOne bug bounty platform, calendar year 2025 (the ninth Hacker-Powered Security Report). Other quotes: "1,121 distinct customer programs included AI in scope in 2025", a "270% increase year over year"; "Valid reports of prompt injection rose 540%". The release makes no measured claim about the productivity of researchers who use AI. — [HackerOne press release](https://www.hackerone.com/press-release/hackerone-report-finds-210-spike-ai-vulnerability-reports-amid-rise-ai-autonomy), 1 Oct 2025. Read: primary, full. Secondary reports add that 49% of the reports from autonomous agents were valid, and that 78% of their valid findings were one simple class of web flaw (cross-site scripting) ([SecurityBrief](https://securitybrief.co.nz/story/ai-vulnerability-reports-surge-as-hackbots-reshape-cyber-risks); search summary only). Quality: reported (platform). Maps to: AI use by skilled researchers (70%); full automation works best on simple, known flaw classes.
- **International AI Safety Report 2026: no fully autonomous end-to-end attacks are reported; at least one real incident used semi-autonomous AI, with humans only at critical decisions.** Covers: a synthesis by more than 100 experts, chaired by Yoshua Bengio, released 3 Feb 2026. Summaries also say that an AI agent placed in the top 5% of teams in a major cyber competition in 2025, and that underground markets sell packaged AI tools that lower the skill needed. — [techUK summary](https://www.techuk.org/insights/news/the-release-of-the-international-ai-safety-report-2026-navigating-rapid-ai-advancement-and-emerging-risks.html), Feb 2026. Read: search summary of secondary reports. Quality: official (international expert synthesis), read through secondary sources. Maps to: AI share of running an attack today (below 100%).

#### Context already in SC45 notes (not read again here)
- Anthropic, GTG-1002 (assessed Chinese state-sponsored, Sep 2025): AI did "80-90% of the campaign"; humans made "perhaps 4-6 critical decision points per hacking campaign"; "roughly thirty global targets" — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage), 13 Nov 2025. The full report gives human steps of 2–10 minutes against 1–4 hours of AI work for vulnerability discovery, and 5–20 minutes against 2–6 hours for data extraction — [Anthropic report PDF](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/ec212e6566a0d47/original/Disrupting-the-first-reported-AI-orchestrated-cyber-espionage-campaign.pdf), as cited in the SC45 effects notes.
- Anthropic, disruptions of Dec 2025 to Aug 2026: "A majority of the operations described in this report were enabled by AI"; "Humans remained in the loop by setting the targets of attacks and reviewing exfiltration"; target counts of more than 20, roughly 50, and 42 organizations for three actors (one actor reached 14 of 42); a cloud takeover in "roughly three hours" — [Anthropic, September 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026).
- Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026 (July 2025 to June 2026): the most complex intrusions "remain manually driven in the majority of campaigns we observe"; the median time from discovery of a flaw in the wild to its weaponization is "well below 24 hours" — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/02/ai-cybersecurity-threats-microsoft-report/), 2 Oct 2026.

### Inferences
- **The SC45 end value for access (87%) already occurs in single frontier cases.** GTG-1002 (80–90% of tactical work, 2025) and JADEPUFFER (end to end, 2026, vendor assessment) are far above the SC45 present value of 25%. But Microsoft, Google and the NCSC all say that the most complex operations stay human-led. So 25% can stand as a force-wide average only if most teams do not yet use agentic tools. A team with frontier tools is near 80–90% for tactical access work on soft targets. All these shares count tasks or activity, not expert labor time.
- **AI-run access to soft targets takes hours.** Cases: about 3 hours from one stolen token to cloud administrator (Anthropic, 2026); under 6 hours for a mass theft of credentials (Google, 2026); a "compressed window" for JADEPUFFER. The 29-minute average breakout for all eCrime in 2025 (CrowdStrike) is of the same order. No case measures AI time against a hardened strategic target. So no AI measurement tests the SC45 present value of 0.55 years, and the end value ("days") is already true for soft targets.
- **Targets per campaign are in the tens.** The 2026 cases give about 20–50 target organizations per actor. No source gives the number of human operators. If a campaign has a few operators, the span is several to tens of targets per operator, above the SC45 present value of 1 attack at once. These are broad campaigns against soft targets, not deliberate strategic attacks with legal review.
- **Use of AI help is near-universal among skilled people; use of autonomous agents is not.** HackerOne's 70% (2025) and CrowdStrike's +89% in one year (2025) measure help and growth. Autonomous end-to-end operations are single cases.

### Gaps
- No source gives operator headcounts per campaign, so I cannot compute targets per operator or expert-hours saved.
- No source measures the share of human labor time (as opposed to tasks) that AI does in a real operation.
- OpenAI's latest threat report (25 Feb 2026) showed no cyber figures in search results, and I did not fetch it ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-malicious-ai-uses)). OpenAI pages refused a plain request in this session (HTTP 403 for a system card).
- BleepingComputer refused a plain request (HTTP 403), so I could not check its JADEPUFFER account. The human role in JADEPUFFER stays uncertain.
- I did not read the full CrowdStrike report or the Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026 itself.

## 2. Benchmarks and evaluations of AI on offensive tasks: success rates, cost, time and trend

### Takeaway
In April 2026, the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) measured 71% success for the best US model on 285 hard capture-the-flag tasks, against 46% for an earlier frontier model and 32% for the best Chinese model. CAISI puts the Chinese frontier about 8 months behind overall (May 2026), and the best open-weight model about 4 months behind on cyber (Sep 2026). The best models now build working end-to-end browser exploits in about 12–14% of attempts, where models of early 2026 were near 0%. OpenAI could not rule out its "Critical" cyber level for one model in August 2026. Test ranges still have no active defenders.

### Cited Findings
- **CAISI (US), April 2026: on 285 difficult capture-the-flag challenges, GPT-5.5 solved 71%, Claude Opus 4.6 46%, GPT-5.4 mini 32% and DeepSeek V4 Pro 32%.** Covers: CTF-Archive-Diamond, "a CAISI-developed benchmark based on 285 difficult CTF challenges" from a platform of Arizona State University; a budget of "1M weighted tokens" per task; the DeepSeek value is "Imputed from a subset of samples via IRT". Quotes: "DeepSeek V4's capabilities lag behind the frontier by about 8 months"; "the U.S. capability frontier tends to lead the PRC frontier by roughly 8 months". The lag covers five domains with equal weight; cyber is one of them. — [NIST CAISI, Evaluation of DeepSeek V4 Pro](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/05/caisi-evaluation-deepseek-v4-pro), released 1 May 2026, updated 2 May 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: official benchmark. Maps to: AI capability in 2026 on single tasks (not operations); the lag for a country without US frontier models (about 8 months).
- **CAISI, September 2026: GLM-5.3 (Zhipu AI, China) is "the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date" and "lags the US frontier by about four months" on CAISI's cyber benchmarks.** — as quoted in [Anthropic, GLM-5.3 and the spread of advanced cyber capabilities](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities), 29 Sep 2026. Read: primary (Anthropic page), full; I did not read CAISI's own page on GLM-5.3. Quality: official benchmark, as reported by an AI developer. Maps to: the lag for any state that uses open-weight models (about 4 months on cyber in late 2026).
- **Anthropic, September 2026: working end-to-end exploits of a browser JavaScript engine in 50 of 410 attempts (GLM-5.3) and 56 of 410 (Claude Mythos Preview); Claude Opus 4.6 and other open models were "at or near 0%".** Covers: ExploitBench on Chrome's V8 engine; the near-zero group is Opus 4.6, GLM-5.2, Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash. On an internal benchmark of 100 random binary tasks, GLM-5.3 reached full control of program flow in 4% of trials and Mythos Preview in 6%. GLM-5.3 was "released without meaningful safeguards to limit misuse": simple techniques made it engage with harmful requests 64% to 100% of the time. Removal of its remaining safeguards took the Anthropic team about 2,200 GPU hours (about $4,400); Anthropic estimates about 600 GPU hours ($1,200) for an experienced team, and others published such versions within days. Quote: "a critical threshold in freely accessible capabilities has now been crossed." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities), 29 Sep 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: benchmark by a competing AI developer (a possible conflict of interest). Maps to: exploit success per attempt at the 2026 frontier (about 12–14%); the cost for any state to get near-frontier cyber capability (about $1,000–$5,000 of compute, beyond free weights).
- **OpenAI cyber range: GPT-5.3-Codex passed 12 of 15 scenarios (80%), against 60% for GPT-5.1-Codex-Max, 53% for GPT-5.2-Codex and 47% for GPT-5.2-Thinking.** It failed the scenarios on evasion of endpoint detection, a certificate and DNS hijack, and a leaked token. Professional capture-the-flag scores were about flat against GPT-5.2-Codex. Reports say OpenAI treated GPT-5.3-Codex as its first model at "High" cyber capability because it cannot rule out that level. — [OpenAI, GPT-5.3-Codex system card](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-3-codex-system-card), 5 Feb 2026, as reported by secondary write-ups such as [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gpt-5-3-codex-release-features-benchmarks-guide). Read: search summary only (OpenAI refused a plain request, HTTP 403). Quality: benchmark (vendor). Maps to: the share of multi-step range scenarios that AI completes (80% in early 2026); evasion of defenses is the main failure.
- **OpenAI levels in 2026: GPT-5.6-Sol "was the first commercial general-purpose model OpenAI rated High for cybersecurity" (preview, June 2026); OpenAI "cannot rule out" that its unreleased model Astra reaches "Critical" (disclosed 10 Aug 2026).** Quotes: "Until this month, no OpenAI model had been assessed above High"; Astra "has not been officially classified as Critical". The note says that a Critical model can "devise and execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies from nothing more than a high-level objective". — [Cloud Security Alliance research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-openai-astra-critical-cyber-threshold-2026/), Aug 2026. Read: secondary, full (no OpenAI primary). Quality: reported. Conflict: other reports say GPT-5.3-Codex (Feb 2026) was the first model treated as High. The two claims may differ between "treated as High as a precaution" and "rated High". Maps to: the date of end-state capability (a model that may plan and run a whole attack from a goal), 2026–2027 for capability, not for adoption.
- **Palisade Research, 2025 competitions: AI teams placed in the top 5% of about 400 teams and in the top 10% of about 8,000 teams.** The abstract says that AI agents can reliably solve challenges that need one hour or less of effort from a median human participant. — [Petrov and Volkov, Evaluating AI cyber capabilities with crowdsourced elicitation, arXiv 2505.19915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19915), 26 May 2025 (events in March 2025). Read: abstract. Quality: benchmark by the developer (claims not reviewed). Maps to: a speed multiple per task (about 11 times) for tasks that the agent can do; the cost multiple is against commercial testing prices.
- **AISI (UK) says that its test environments without defenders will soon stop telling the best models apart; future ranges will add active monitoring and incident response.** — [AISI, Our evaluation of Claude Mythos Preview's cyber capabilities](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities), Apr 2026. Read: search summary only (the SC45 notes read this page in full for other figures). #### Context already in SC45 notes (not read again here)
- AISI: the 80%-reliability cyber task length doubled every 4.7 months (estimate of Feb 2026; 8 months in the estimate of Nov 2025); a newer Mythos Preview checkpoint completed all six tasks of 8 hours or more, a 32-step network range (about 20 expert hours) in 6 of 10 attempts, and an industrial control range in 3 of 10 (the first success there) — [AISI](https://aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing), 13 May 2026.

### Inferences
- **The human-time size of reliable AI work grew about 8 times in about 14 months.** Reliable tasks went from about 1 hour of median-human effort (Palisade, March 2025) to 8 hours or more of expert work (AISI, May 2026). That is 3 doublings in about 14 months, or one doubling every 4–5 months (my calculation). The human groups differ (median participants against experts), so the true rate is probably faster. The result agrees with AISI's 4.7 months.
- **Exploit success went from near 0% to about 14% per attempt in about 2 months at the frontier, and open weights followed about 5 months later.** On ExploitBench (measured in Sep 2026), Opus 4.6 (released Feb 2026) was near 0%, Mythos Preview (Apr 2026) about 14%, and the open-weight GLM-5.3 (Sep 2026) about 12% (dates are model release dates). Success per attempt is the useful unit when compute is cheap, because an operator can run many attempts.
- **A state without US frontier models trails by under one year.** CAISI's lag of about 8 months overall (May 2026) and about 4 months on cyber for the best open model (Sep 2026) imply that any state with modest compute gets frontier cyber capability of today within a year. SC45 can give a second state the same AI path with a delay of under one year.
- **Capability for the SC45 end state may arrive before 2030; adoption is a separate question.** OpenAI could not rule out its Critical level in August 2026. Capability alone does not set the share of work in a state force (see section 4 on authorization).

### Gaps
- No public evaluation tests models against defended ranges with active monitoring and response. Every range-based share transfers from settings without defenders.
- I found no AISI evaluation of Claude Mythos 5 or GPT-5.6 Sol. A news flash claims an AISI report of 17 Jul 2026 with a 70-task range and a capability gap of "4 to 7 months" ([KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/gpt-5-6-sol-outperforms-mythos-in-cybersecurity-capabilities-open-source-models-close-gap); search summary only). Another analysis says no such side-by-side evaluation exists. Treat the claim as unverified.
- OpenAI's system cards refused plain requests (HTTP 403), so the cyber range numbers are secondary.
- Few sources give the compute cost of one range run. CAISI gives token budgets, not spend.

## 3. AI in vulnerability discovery and exploit development: finds per researcher or per dollar, and time to exploit

### Takeaway
Users of frontier AI report more than ten times their former rate of finding flaws (May 2026), and partners of Anthropic's Project Glasswing verified at least 129,000 flaws in four months (April–July 2026). Human triage and patching are now the bottleneck: under 1% of Glasswing findings were fixed by September 2026, and AI rated about 91% of its findings critical or high where maintainers rated about 51%. Field exploitation has not yet sped up: in the first half of 2026, 23% of newly exploited flaws were exploited on or before disclosure (29% in 2025), and flaws that AI found are exploited at about the same rate as others (1.3%).

### Cited Findings

#### Discovery volume and the human review bottleneck
- **Anthropic, Feb 2026: Claude Opus 4.6 found and validated "more than 500 high-severity vulnerabilities" in open-source code, without custom tools or prompts.** Covers: memory corruption flaws in projects such as GhostScript, OpenSC and CGIF; some had been in heavily fuzzed code for decades. Humans validated every flaw and at first wrote the patches by hand. Quotes: "Industry-standard 90-day windows may not hold up against the speed and volume of LLM-discovered bugs"; models "may soon exceed the speed and scale of even expert human researchers". The page gives no cost, compute, time or false-positive rate. — [Anthropic, zero-days research post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/zero-days), 5 Feb 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: reported (developer's own test). Maps to: the discovery multiplier before Mythos-class models.
- **Glasswing, May 2026: "Several have told us that their rate of bug-finding has increased by more than a factor of ten"; Mozilla found 271 Firefox flaws, "over ten times more" than with Claude Opus 4.6.** Covers: Project Glasswing partners with Claude Mythos Preview, in the first month after 7 Apr 2026. Other figures: partners found "more than ten thousand" high- or critical-severity flaws; Cloudflare found 2,000 bugs, 400 of them high or critical. Anthropic's scan of more than 1,000 open-source projects gave 23,019 findings, of which 6,202 are estimated high or critical. Of 1,752 findings that six independent firms or Anthropic assessed, 90.6% (1,587) were true positives and 62.4% (1,094) were confirmed high or critical. Of 530 disclosed high or critical flaws, 75 were patched; a patch took about two weeks on average. Quote: "the bottleneck in fixing bugs like these is the human capacity to triage, report, and design and deploy patches for them." — [Anthropic, Project Glasswing: An initial update](https://www.anthropic.com/news/glasswing-initial-update), 22 May 2026. Read: primary, full. Engadget reports the same figures ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/2180028/anthropic-claude-mythos-preview-project-glasswing-update), 23 May 2026; Read: secondary, full). Quality: reported (developer; the factor of ten is a partner statement with no stated baseline). Maps to: the discovery multiplier per researcher (more than 10 times for frontier users in 2026); the validation rate (about 91% true, about 62% at the claimed severity).
- **Cyber Verification Program, Oct 2026: partners found "at least 129,000 verified software vulnerabilities between April and July 2026", and Anthropic's own scan found "an additional 5,500" between April and October 2026.** More than 33,000 are rated critical or high. The data come from "33 partner reports". Anthropic says that the true count is probably at least five times higher, and "fewer than 50% of partners disclosed patched numbers". The program now covers Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.1, in three access tiers. — [Anthropic, Expanding the Cyber Verification Program](https://www.anthropic.com/news/cyber-verification-program), 6 Oct 2026; [Project Glasswing page](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing), updated 6 Oct 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: reported (developer, from partner self-reports). Maps to: the discovery rate at the 2026 frontier (about 3,900 verified flaws per partner report in four months; my calculation, 129,000 ÷ 33; the partners' sizes are not given).
- **VulnCheck audit, Sep 2026: of 26,153 Glasswing findings, 2,736 (10.5%) reached Anthropic's public disclosure record and 202 (0.8%) are fixed, in 113 projects.** Other figures: 2,096 (8%) disclosed but not confirmed fixed; 245 (0.9%) withdrawn; 18 were already patched before Anthropic reported them. Claude rated 91.5% of the findings critical or high; maintainers rated 51.3% critical or high. Anthropic's dashboard shows 421 patched findings and 462 advisories, against 202 fixed findings in the record. VulnCheck quotes Anthropic: "independent human triage and review is the rate limiting step". — [VulnCheck, Patrick Garrity, The Anthropic Glasswing receipts are starting to trickle in](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/anthropic-glasswing-receipts), 8 Sep 2026. Read: primary, full. Quality: independent analysis by a vendor of vulnerability data. Maps to: the supervision and validation cost of AI discovery output; AI overstates severity (about 91% against about 51%).

### Inferences
- **The SC45 end value of 10 times for finds of new flaws is already reported in 2026 by some frontier users.** Glasswing partners report more than ten times their former rate (May 2026), and Mozilla's count rose more than ten times between two model generations. So 10 times is a present value for a researcher with frontier tools, not an end value, and the end value could be much higher. The partner reports give no baseline and have no independent check.
- **Usable finds are a small part of raw finds.** About 10% of Glasswing findings reached maintainers and under 1% were fixed by September 2026. AI rated about 91% of its findings critical or high where maintainers rated about 51%. If SC45 counts finds that an operator can use, the multiplier on validated, usable flaws is lower than on raw finds, and human triage limits it. No source gives triage hours per finding.
- **Exploits lag finds.** At the 2026 frontier, about 12–14% of attempts give a working end-to-end exploit for a hard target (a browser engine). An exploit chain for a known flaw costs about $20 of compute and 20 minutes of expert time. For an operator, the compute cost is small against the labor cost.
- **Field data do not yet show faster exploitation.** The share of flaws exploited on or before disclosure fell in the first half of 2026 (23.4% against 28.9%). The count of flaws exploited within 31 days stayed at about 200 per half-year. Attackers use AI-found flaws at about the same rate as other flaws (about 1.3%). Only the median time to known-exploited listing fell (120 to 80 days). Lab capability is far ahead of field behavior; attackers may not need new flaws while old flaws still work.

### Gaps
- No source gives flaws found per researcher-year with and without AI, with headcounts. The factor of ten is a partner statement.
- No independent count of Glasswing results exists, other than VulnCheck's audit of the public record.
- I found no Mandiant time-to-exploit value for 2026, and no field data that split state from criminal exploitation.
- The Zero Day Clock site did not answer (HTTP 503), so its values come from a critic and a low-quality news report.

## 4. Projections: AI share of offensive cyber work in 5–25 years and operator productivity

### Takeaway
I found no credible numeric forecast of the share of offensive cyber work that AI will do 5–25 years ahead, or of operator productivity multiples. The available forecasts are short-range (the NCSC to 2027: fully automated end-to-end advanced attacks are unlikely; Gartner: AI agents halve the time to exploit account exposures by 2027), statements by AI developers that capabilities spread within months, and one expert-panel date: public authorization of autonomous cyber operations by NATO or a member state at a median of 2041 (experts) or 2038 (superforecasters). Together they support a fast path for capability and a slower path for adoption in a state force.

- **Capability and adoption need separate paths.** Every measured capability path in 2025–2026 is fast (doublings every 4–5 months, frontier exploit success from near 0% to about 14% in about 2 months). The only forecast that touches adoption in a state force puts public authorization of autonomous cyber operations near 2038–2041. If SC45 uses one path for the share of work, it mixes the two; the evidence supports a fast path for discovery and access capability and a slower path for the running and effect shares.
- **Access to the frontier is a separate input.** The best cyber model of mid-2026 is restricted to selected partners, while an open-weight model is about 4 months behind. A state's AI share depends on the models it can use, and that lag is under one year for any state with modest compute.
