# AI uplift for intelligence and military targeting analysts

Notes for SC45 (a country's cyber attacks against surveillance, 2026–2051). The model now assumes, for planning (target analysis, legal review, approval), 1 expert-year per attack plus 0.5 per target, with AI doing 0% today and 20% at the end of AI progress. For damage assessment it assumes 1 expert-year per attack plus 0.7 per target, with AI doing 0% today and 30% at the end, on a slower path. Each finding gives its date, how much of the source I read, its quality (official, reported, estimate, experiment, opinion, unverified) and the model input it can inform. "My calculation" marks arithmetic that the source does not state. All figures are coarse; I left out method detail.

## 1. Targeting throughput with AI decision support: what share of target analysis does AI do, and what multiple of throughput per analyst?

### Takeaway
Leading forces already use AI for a large part of target analysis in kinetic targeting. The reported gains per person are one to two orders of magnitude: about 20 people instead of more than 2,000 in a US Army targeting cell (exercise, 2024), and about 100 targets a day instead of about 50 a year in Israel (2021). Most of the gain comes from detection, data fusion and digital workflow, not from judgment. No source measures the AI share of analyst hours directly, and public evidence for offensive cyber target analysis is thin.

### Cited Findings

#### United States: Maven Smart System, XVIII Airborne Corps, NGA and CENTCOM
- **About 20 people matched the output of a targeting cell of more than 2,000.** The CSET case study (Emelia Probasco, "Building the Tech Coalition", August 2024) says the XVIII Airborne Corps matched the 2003 Operation Iraqi Freedom time-critical targeting cell, which CSET calls "widely viewed as the most efficient in U.S. military history", with about 20 people. The 2003 cell had more than 2,000 staff. — [Army Times, Todd South, 21 Aug 2024](https://development.armytimes.com/?p=50904); [Breaking Defense, Theresa Hitchens, 22 May 2025](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/05/ai-unchained-ngas-maven-tool-significantly-decreasing-time-to-targeting-agency-chief-says/); [CSET summary page](https://cset.georgetown.edu/?p=19325) ("two thousand fewer servicemembers"). *Date: 2024 exercise result against a 2003 baseline; Read: news articles in full, the CSET PDF not opened; Quality: case study with corps access, reported by news; Maps to: throughput per analyst in target analysis. My calculation: ×100 output per person, so, if the workload is the same, humans do about 1% of the 2003 effort per target. Digital workflow and data integration explain part of this, not only AI.*
- **One "digital target pass" fell from 743 minutes (late 2020) to under one minute (2024).** The first Scarlet Dragon experiment (late 2020, XVIII Airborne Corps with II Marine Expeditionary Force) took more than 12 hours per pass. There were at least 10 rounds by August 2024. CSET: without Maven "the firing process is manual and riddled with inefficiencies and the potential for errors". — [Army Times, 21 Aug 2024](https://development.armytimes.com/?p=50904). *Date: 2020 → 2024; Read: full; Quality: exercise data, reported; Maps to: time per target in the machine-to-machine handoff (more than ×700 faster, my calculation), not analyst-years.*
- **Goal: 1,000 targeting decisions an hour.** CSET: "The ultimate goal is for the system and soldiers to be able to help a commander process 1,000 tactical decisions an hour." NGA Director Vice Adm. Frank Whitworth repeated the Army goal of 1,000 "high-quality" decisions (choosing and dismissing targets) in one hour at GEOINT 2025. — [Army Times](https://development.armytimes.com/?p=50904); [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/05/ai-unchained-ngas-maven-tool-significantly-decreasing-time-to-targeting-agency-chief-says/). *Date: Aug 2024, May 2025; Read: full; Quality: official goal, not a measured rate; Maps to: end-state throughput. Some outlets misreport it as 1,000 targets struck per hour ([Indian Defence Review](https://indiandefencereview.com/the-u-s-army-deploys-ai-capable-of-striking-1000-targets-per-hour/)); the officials' words do not support that.*
- **NGA: Maven "decreased targeting workflow timelines by a substantial amount".** In one recent, unnamed exercise, timelines from sensing to target engagement fell "from hours to minutes". Maven had about 20,000 active users across more than 35 tools of the services and commands, in three security domains. Users more than quadrupled after March 2024 and more than doubled after January 2025. CDAO gave Palantir $513 million in 2024 to expand Maven at combatant commands; the Army bought $795 million in licenses in May 2025. — [Breaking Defense, 22 May 2025](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/05/ai-unchained-ngas-maven-tool-significantly-decreasing-time-to-targeting-agency-chief-says/). *Date: May 2025; Read: full; Quality: official statement; Maps to: time per target (my reading of "hours to minutes": ×10 to ×100). The user count shows that the AI share today is above zero in US targeting.*
- **CENTCOM, February 2024: Maven computer vision helped narrow targets for more than 85 US air strikes on 2 February 2024.** CENTCOM CTO Schuyler Moore: "Every step that involves AI has a human checking in at the end." She said the AI recommendation engine "frequently fell short" when it suggested weapons and attack plans. — [The Register, 27 Feb 2024, from Bloomberg, 26 Feb 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/02/27/us_military_maven_ai_used/). *Read: full; Quality: official statements, reported; Maps to: in 2024 the AI share of detection and nomination was real, and the AI share of weapon and plan choice was low.*
- **Project Convergence, 2020: Army officials said AI and autonomy cut the sensor-to-shooter time from about 20 minutes to about 20 seconds.** Gen. Mike Murray called the result "some success", not a routine capability, and Janes reported misfires. — [Defense News, 25 Sep 2020](https://www.defensenews.com/artificial-intelligence/2020/09/25/the-army-just-conducted-a-massive-test-of-its-battlefield-artificial-intelligence-in-the-desert/); [Breaking Defense, Sep 2020](https://breakingdefense.com/2020/09/target-gone-in-20-seconds-army-sensor-shooter-test/). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official claim in a demonstration; Maps to: time in dynamic targeting (×60), not deliberate planning.*

#### United States: war with Iran, 2026 (reported)
- **About 1,000 targets in the first 24 hours, which the US military called double the 2003 "shock and awe" campaign.** "Targeting that once took hours or days now takes minutes", according to military officials. CDAO Cameron Stanley described a few clicks in one system from target identification to strike execution. Maven "recommends targets that humans are supposed to verify". — [KOLD/AZFamily, Derek Staahl, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.kold.com/2026/03/26/ai-targeting-system-doubles-pace-us-strikes-iran/). *Read: full; Quality: official claims, reported; Maps to: throughput (×2 against 2003 on the first day, not per analyst) and time per target (hours or days → minutes).*
- **More than 13,000 targets struck in the first 38 days (White House accounting, 8 Apr 2026).** Senior officials said the Pentagon used Maven to identify top-priority targets and help choose weapons. Stanley said Maven replaced "eight or nine systems" with one view. CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper said AI helps sort data "in seconds". — [Arms Control Today, Michael Klare, May 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-05/news/ai-plays-major-role-war-iran). *Read: full; Quality: official totals, AI role reported; Maps to: sustained throughput of about 340 targets a day (my calculation, 13,000 ÷ 38). The source gives no analyst counts, so there is no per-analyst multiple.*
- **Reported first-day counts conflict.** Reports give about 1,000 targets in 24 hours (Washington Post, as summarized), about 900 strikes in 12 hours, and an outlier of 3,000 targets. Reports say Anthropic's Claude ran inside Maven and suggested and ranked targets; Anthropic and Palantir declined to comment. — [India TV News, 7 Mar 2026](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/world/how-us-military-used-ai-to-attack-iran-and-hit-1-000-targets-within-first-24-hours-details-of-war-in-middle-east-2026-03-07-1032911); [Anadolu Agency](https://aa.com.tr/en/americas/us-used-ai-powered-system-to-identify-targets-in-iran-report/3851248). *Read: search summary only; Quality: secondhand, anonymous sources; Maps to: order of magnitude only.*
- **A claim that Maven "can generate 1,000 targeting recommendations per hour" is unverified.** The Outpost attributes it to Whitworth at Palantir's AIPCon 9 (March 2026), via The Register. I could not find The Register's original. Arms Control Today reports CDAO Stanley's remarks at AIPCon 9 and does not give this figure. — [The Outpost](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/pentagon-locks-in-palantir-ai-as-core-us-military-system-with-maven-program-of-record-designation-24791/). *Quality: unverified secondary.*

#### Israel: Gospel and Lavender (reported; the IDF disputes parts)
- **Aviv Kohavi (IDF chief of staff to 2023): in the May 2021 war the Gospel produced about 100 targets a day, and about half were attacked; before, the IDF produced about 50 targets a year in Gaza.** The target division had "hundreds of officers or soldiers". — Wikipedia summary of [The Guardian, 1 Dec 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/dec/01/the-gospel-how-israel-uses-ai-to-select-bombing-targets) and [The Conversation, 8 Dec 2023](https://theconversation.com/israels-ai-can-produce-100-bombing-targets-a-day-in-gaza-is-this-the-future-of-war-219302), in [Wikipedia, "AI-assisted targeting in the Gaza Strip"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_targeting_in_the_Gaza_Strip). *Date: a 2023 quote about 2021; Read: Wikipedia, first 100,000 characters (the Guardian was blocked to the fetch tool); Quality: claim by a named official, reported; Maps to: target-generation rate ×730 (my calculation, 100 a day against 50 a year). Team sizes differ and are not known, so this is not a per-analyst multiple.*
- **Tal Mimran (lecturer, former IDF legal adviser) estimated that 20 intelligence officers produced 50–100 targets in 300 days; the Gospel and related systems can suggest about 200 targets in 10–12 days.** — [NPR, Geoff Brumfiel, 14 Dec 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/12/14/1218643254/israel-is-using-an-ai-system-to-find-targets-in-gaza-experts-say-its-just-the-st), via the Wikipedia article above (NPR returned HTTP 503 to the fetch tool). *Quality: estimate by a named expert; Maps to: human-only work per target. My calculation: 20 × 300/365 ≈ 16 officer-years for 50–100 targets, so 0.16–0.33 analyst-years per target before AI. If the same 20 officers made 200 targets in 10–12 days, that is about 0.003 analyst-years per target, a multiple of about ×50 to ×120. The team size with the Gospel is not reported.*
- **IDF figures, late 2023.** More than 12,000 targets were identified by the target administration division by early November 2023 (IDF statements, via The Guardian). The IDF struck up to 250 targets a day and more than 22,000 targets by 14 December 2023 (NPR). In 2021, the Gospel made about 200 of the targets among about 1,500 strikes. The IDF said the Gospel lets it use "automatic tools to produce targets at a fast pace". — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_targeting_in_the_Gaza_Strip) of the Guardian and NPR. *Quality: official figures, reported; Maps to: throughput.*
- **Lavender: about 37,000 people marked; sources put its accuracy at about 90% (from a sample check).** One officer said he spent about "20 seconds" on each target, and sources described officers as a "rubber stamp". Before the war, human teams handled lists of a few dozen senior operatives. The sources were six unnamed intelligence officers. The IDF denies a kill list, calls the tools "auxiliary" and says an analyst must examine each target independently. — [+972 Magazine and Local Call, Yuval Abraham, 3 Apr 2024](https://www.972mag.com/lavender-ai-israeli-army-gaza/). *Date: 2023–24; Read: full; Quality: reported, anonymous sources, disputed by the IDF; Maps to: human approval time per target (about 20 seconds) when AI supplies the targets and policy allows it; an error rate of about 10%.*
- **The Washington Post (late December 2024, as summarized by others) reported that when the target bank ran low, the IDF used the Gospel to make hundreds of new targets to keep the pace.** Sources said findings needed confirmation by two independent sources. — [Yeni Şafak, citing Anadolu](https://yenisafak.com/en/news/israels-ai-system-used-in-gaza-attacks-has-decades-of-history-report-3696513). *Read: search summary of secondary coverage only (the Post is paywalled); Quality: reported, not checked here; Maps to: AI used where human target development is the bottleneck.*

#### Ukraine: DELTA and Avengers (official Ukrainian claims)
- **Avengers, the AI module in DELTA, detects up to 12,000 pieces of Russian equipment a week** (Ministry of Defence, Deputy Minister Kateryna Chernohorenko, 23 Sep 2024). — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-ato/3908891-thanks-to-ai-ukrainian-military-detect-12000-targets-every-week.html). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official claim, no independent check; Maps to: detection throughput.*
- **Automatic detection identifies 70% of unique enemy equipment units, with an average detection time of 2.2 seconds; DELTA has verified more than 130,000 destroyed or damaged Russian targets, about 25% of them personnel** (Denys Shmyhal, 8 Oct 2025; he signed the order for DELTA across the forces in August 2025). — [United24, 8 Oct 2025](https://united24media.com/latest-news/ai-powered-battle-network-ukraines-delta-fully-deployed-marking-130000-verified-russian-targets-12300); [Euromaidan Press, 8 Oct 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/10/08/data-driven-warfare-ukraines-delta-system-verifies-130000-russian-targets-in-two-months/). *Read: full; Quality: official claim; conflict: Euromaidan says "two months" after deployment across the forces, United24 says "since its launch", and United24 calls Shmyhal prime minister while Euromaidan calls him defence minister; Maps to: an AI share of about 0.7 in 2025 for one narrow subtask (detection), and damage verification at scale (see question 3).*

### Inferences
- In kinetic targeting the AI share of target analysis today is not zero. The US (2024–26), Israel (2021–24) and Ukraine (2024–25) report routine AI detection, nomination and ranking of targets. For one narrow subtask, Ukraine reports that AI finds 70% of the units.
- Reported multiples run from ×2 (Iran first day against 2003, not per person) through ×50 to ×120 (Gaza, if the team size held) to ×100 per person (XVIII Airborne exercise). They mix AI with digital workflow, policy change and the acceptance of more error. Treat them as upper bounds on the part due to AI.
- The human-only baseline of 0.16–0.33 analyst-years per target (Mimran's numbers, my calculation) is of the same order as SC45's 0.5 expert-years per target. Cyber targets probably need more technical analysis per target, so 0.5 is plausible for a human-only baseline.
- For SC45, the per-target part of planning (target analysis) can take a much larger AI share than the per-attack part (legal review, approval). For a leading cyber power, a per-target AI share of about 0.1–0.3 today and 0.6–0.9 at the end fits the kinetic evidence better than 0 → 0.2. This is an assumption by transfer: the kinetic systems process imagery and other sensor data, and cyber target analysis is not public.
- The error evidence (Lavender misidentification of about 10%, as reported; the Minab school strike in 2026; Moore's "frequently fell short" for plan recommendations) suggests that the AI share rises faster for volume than for quality.

### Gaps
- No source measures the share of analyst hours that AI does in a targeting cell. All multiples are output comparisons with confounders.
- I found no public figure for AI in offensive cyber target analysis (network mapping, target system analysis) by CYBERCOM, NSA or other states.
- Team sizes with the Gospel, and in US targeting cells in the 2026 Iran war, are not public.
- I did not open the CSET PDF; the "20 against 2,000" wording comes from news coverage and the CSET summary page.
- I did not read the Washington Post (Dec 2024; Mar 2026) or WSJ originals (paywalls); those claims come from secondary reports.

## 2. Legal review and approval: how long do approvals take, and does AI shorten them or do they stay human-bound?

### Takeaway
Policy, not AI, sets the approval time for cyber operations. CIA cyber covert-action approvals fell from "as much as a year or more" to "a matter of weeks" after a 2018 presidential finding, and NSPM-13 (2018) moved interagency review out of the National Security Council. I found no source that shows AI shortens US legal review or approval; US policy and officials keep a human check at the end. The Israeli reports show what can happen when AI supplies targets in bulk under permissive policy: human approval per target fell to about 20 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- **CIA cyber covert action: approval times fell from "as much as a year or more" to "a matter of weeks" after a 2018 presidential finding.** The finding removed case-by-case NSC screening for operations against named countries (Russia, China, North Korea, Iran). CIA then carried out "more than a dozen operations" (Yahoo News, Dorfman, Zetter, McLaughlin and Naylor, as summarized by Robert Chesney). — [Lawfare, 15 Jul 2020](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/cia-covert-action-and-operations-cyberspace). *Read: full; Quality: reported (named reporters, anonymous officials); Maps to: per-attack approval time. My reading: about 52 weeks → a few weeks, a cut of about ×10 or more, caused by delegation, not AI.*
- **NSPM-13 (2018) replaced PPD-20.** Under PPD-20 an "interagency process ... went through the National Security Council and all the way up", and any participant could halt it. NSPM-13 moved coordination to a DoD-led process. Former Cyber Command general counsel Gary Corn said it enables faster, more agile decisions. The text is classified and gives no public timing data. The Biden administration reviewed it from 2021. — [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ppd-20-grant-schneider-cyber-command/); [CyberScoop](https://www.cyberscoop.com/biden-trump-nspm-13-presidential-memo-cyber-command-white-house/); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/president-bidens-policy-changes-offensive-cyber-operations). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official statements, reported; Maps to: per-attack approval time depends on delegation.*
- **Operation GLOWING SYMPHONY (2016): the plan drew "several" interagency non-concurs, which led to NSC Deputies and Principals Committee meetings.** The Archive's summary of Washington Post reporting says objections "required weeks of NSC meeting to iron out". Authorization came on 10 November 2016 for a 30-day window, which was later extended. The 30-day assessment says that the time to elevate and negotiate the non-concurs prevented part of the plan "as originally designed". NPR reported that the task force spent months preparing. — [National Security Archive, 21 Jan 2020](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/cyber-vault/2020-01-21/uscybercom-after-action-assessments-operation-glowing-symphony); [WWNO/NPR, 26 Sep 2019](https://www.wwno.org/npr-news/2019-09-26/how-the-u-s-cracked-into-one-of-the-most-secretive-terrorist-organizations). *Read: Archive page full, NPR by search summary; Quality: declassified official documents and reported; Maps to: per-attack approval time of weeks under PPD-20, with months of preparation.*
- **US policy keeps human judgment.** DoD Directive 3000.09 (25 Jan 2023) requires that systems let commanders and operators exercise "appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force". CNAS notes that this wording has not changed since 2012; "appropriate" is a flexible term. — [DoD issuance PDF](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodd/300009p.PDF); [CNAS, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.cnas.org/press/press-note/noteworthy-dod-autonomous-weapons-policy). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official policy; Maps to: the AI share of legal review and approval stays low by rule.*
- **Reports in 2026 say the US is loosening this rule.** The January 2026 AI strategy reportedly uses a generic "any lawful use" clause, and a White House memo reportedly orders a review of 3000.09 within 90 days. — [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/news/who-controls-the-kill-chain-the-pentagons-ai-rules-dont-agree/); [Let's Data Science, citing Just Security](https://letsdatascience.com/news/pentagon-faces-call-to-update-autonomous-weapons-definitions-5cdbce15). *Read: search summary only; Quality: unverified secondary; Maps to: a possible faster rise of the AI share in approval after 2026.*
- **CENTCOM, 2024: "Every step that involves AI has a human checking in at the end." "There is never an algorithm that's just running, coming to a conclusion and then pushing onto the next step."** — [The Register, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/02/27/us_military_maven_ai_used/). *Read: full; Quality: official statement; Maps to: approval stays human.*
- **Iran, 2026: human officers "supposedly" review every target before a strike order.** Nilza Amaral (Chatham House) warns that automation bias can make approval "a mere formality". A preliminary CENTCOM assessment says the Minab school, where more than 170 people died, went onto an AI-generated target list without adequate human supervision; officials told the New York Times it was "human error in wartime". At a Senate hearing a general said commanders must comply with the law of armed conflict. — [Arms Control Today, May 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-05/news/ai-plays-major-role-war-iran); [KOLD/AZFamily, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.kold.com/2026/03/26/ai-targeting-system-doubles-pace-us-strikes-iran/). *Read: full; Quality: reported; Maps to: human review becomes the bottleneck and the failure point when AI raises volume.*
- **Israel: about 20 seconds of human review per Lavender target (one officer); "less than a minute on a target" (Abraham on France 24).** — [+972 Magazine, 3 Apr 2024](https://www.972mag.com/lavender-ai-israeli-army-gaza/); [France 24, 12 Dec 2023](https://www.france24.com/en/tv-shows/perspective/20231212-understanding-how-israel-uses-gospel-ai-system-in-gaza-bombings) (via Wikipedia). *Quality: reported, disputed by the IDF; Maps to: per-target approval work can fall to almost zero when policy accepts it.*
- **Opinion, June 2026: an analyst can get machine-generated correlations in seconds but still "wait hours or days" for cross-agency coordination, source validation or product approval.** — [The Cipher Brief, Geof Kahn, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/ai-is-speeding-up-intelligence-but-not-the-system-around-it). *Read: full; Quality: opinion of a former CIA and HPSCI official, no data; Maps to: approval as the remaining human-bound step.*

### Inferences
- In SC45 terms, the per-attack term (1 expert-year) is mostly legal review, interagency coordination and approval. Its elapsed time (weeks to a year) depends on policy. Its AI share today is about 0. An end value of 0–0.2 fits US policy as written in 2023.
- Delegation changed approval time more than AI did: about ×10 (a year → weeks) in 2018. A model can treat a change of authorities as a separate step change, not as AI progress. It changes elapsed time more than expert-years.
- Per-target legal checks are different. Under permissive policy and AI volume, per-target review fell to seconds (Israel, reported). If SC45 counts per-target legal checks inside the 0.5 expert-years, that part can shrink with AI, but at a cost in errors.
- No source gives expert-years for approval. My reading is that approval uses few expert-years relative to target analysis, and matters more for elapsed time and tempo.

### Gaps
- No public figure for NSPM-13 approval times, or for the expert-years of legal review per cyber operation.
- No source shows AI tools in US military legal review (judge advocates) of targets or cyber operations.
- I found no GAO or CRS figure for strike approval times (for example, hours per deliberate target) in recent US campaigns.

## 3. Battle damage assessment (kinetic and cyber): time per assessment, share automated, and cyber damage assessment

### Takeaway
First-phase kinetic damage assessment is becoming automated. Ukraine reports verification of more than 130,000 destroyed or damaged targets, and 2026 US reporting says new target lists follow damage assessment "in a matter of minutes". Deep assessment still takes days to weeks: for the Iranian nuclear sites in 2025, a preliminary assessment came in about two days, and the final one was not done after three weeks. Cyber damage assessment is slower and immature: Cyber Command assessed GLOWING SYMPHONY at about 30 and 120 days and reported difficulty in measuring overall impact.

### Cited Findings
- **US Air Force (2026 SBIR topic): today, BDA after-action reports come "hours after mission execution" and need several imagery collections; this "does not meet timeline requirements".** The Air Force seeks real-time automated combat assessment. — [HigherGov listing of the DAF26 topic](https://www.highergov.com/contract-opportunity/automated-combat-assessment-at-the-edge-daf26tz06-nv007-sbir-4dfc9/). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official solicitation; Maps to: time per kinetic assessment today (hours); the Air Force does not yet have automatic BDA in routine use.*
- **Iranian nuclear sites, June 2025: a "preliminary, low confidence" DIA assessment came out within about two days; nearly three weeks later the official analysis was still pending.** Gen. Dan Caine: "Final battle damage will take some time." — [Washington Times, 10 Jul 2025](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/jul/10/pentagon-still-waiting-official-damage-assessment-iran-nuke-strike/); [Task & Purpose](https://taskandpurpose.com/news/operation-midnight-hammer-assessment-iran/). *Read: search summary only; Quality: official statements, reported; Maps to: time for a deep (functional) assessment of hard targets, in weeks.*
- **Iran, 2026: after damage assessment, commanders can make new target lists "in a matter of minutes".** — [Arms Control Today, May 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-05/news/ai-plays-major-role-war-iran). *Read: full; Quality: reported; Maps to: the re-attack loop after first-phase assessment is now fast.*
- **Ukraine, October 2025: DELTA verified more than 130,000 destroyed or damaged Russian targets.** — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/10/08/data-driven-warfare-ukraines-delta-system-verifies-130000-russian-targets-in-two-months/); [United24](https://united24media.com/latest-news/ai-powered-battle-network-ukraines-delta-fully-deployed-marking-130000-verified-russian-targets-12300). *Read: full; Quality: official claim; the period conflicts (two months, or since launch) and the AI share of verification is not stated; Maps to: a high volume of tactical assessment in a digital force.*
- **Past bottleneck: in Desert Storm, "DIA conducted BDA on only 357 of the 862 strategic targets" (41%), and BDA "was neither as timely nor as complete as planners had apparently assumed".** — [GAO/NSIAD-97-134, June 1997](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-97-134/html/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-97-134.htm) (also in learning_assessment.md in this folder). *Quality: official audit (older); Maps to: assessment capacity as a binding limit when targets are many.*
- **Other efforts give no timing figures.** Scale AI used machine learning on satellite imagery in Ukraine to find damage faster and more accurately than human reporting ([Defense One, Jan 2023](https://defenseone.com/technology/2023/01/how-ai-could-predict-damage-ukraine-russian-missiles/381633)). BlackSky has contracts for AI damage assessment from space ([The Defense Post, 9 Jul 2026](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/07/09/blacksky-gen3-ai-battle-assessment/)). A 2021 Air Force SBIR award aimed for algorithmic BDA reports in under 24 hours after imagery arrives ([Inknowvation](https://www.inknowvation.com/sbir/awards/af-2021-machine-learning-automated-weapon-selection-battle-damage-assessments)). *Read: search summaries only; Quality: vendor and official development goals.*
- **Cyber: Cyber Command's GLOWING SYMPHONY assessments are dated 13 December 2016 ("30-Day") and 12 April 2017 ("120-Day"), 33 and 153 days after the 10 November 2016 authorization (my calculation).** The 30-day assessment says the operation "imposed time and resource costs" on ISIS and "successfully contested ISIL in the information domain". It also reports difficulty in measuring the overall impact on ISIS media, and says collection assets had to be ready before the start. The 120-day assessment names data storage as a key obstacle to the use of collected data. The redacted reports do not show whether the wider effect was reached. — [National Security Archive, 21 Jan 2020](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/cyber-vault/2020-01-21/uscybercom-after-action-assessments-operation-glowing-symphony); [The Defense Post, 21 Jan 2020](https://thedefensepost.com/2020/01/21/us-cybercom-glowing-symphony-isis/amp/). *Read: Archive page full; Quality: official, declassified, redacted; Maps to: time per campaign-level cyber assessment of 1–5 months; the AI share was 0 in 2016.*
- **Cyber damage assessment has no mature method.** A NATO STO paper (2025) says that, unlike kinetic warfare, cyber lacks equally mature BDA methods, and that no model yet gives a comprehensive damage assessment. Academic work says cyber effects can be reversible, non-linear and hard to attribute. — [NATO STO paper (Dondo, 2025)](https://review.sto.nato.int/images/Papers/2-Dondo-sas-spring-2025.pdf); [Denning, NPS, "Assessing Cyber War"](https://faculty.nps.edu/dedennin/publications/Assessing%20Cyber%20War.pdf). *Read: search summary only; Quality: expert analysis; Maps to: a slower AI path for cyber assessment.*
- **Cyber Command (defensive work): AI cut analysis of malicious network traffic "that once took hours, days, or weeks" to "minutes and hours"** (Executive Director Morgan Adamski, 2 Apr 2025). — [CyberScoop, 2 Apr 2025](https://cyberscoop.com/cyber-command-ai-gains-cybersecurity-network-monitoring/). *Read: full; Quality: official, qualitative; Maps to: about ×10 less time for network-data analysis (my reading), the closest public analogue for cyber damage assessment work.*

### Inferences
- Damage assessment has two parts. (a) First-phase checks per target, which AI already speeds a lot for imagery. (b) Campaign-level assessment of effect, which takes weeks to months and stays human and limited by data.
- For cyber, the per-target work (confirm the effect on each target system) can use the same network-data analysis that Cyber Command says AI already speeds about ×10 in defense. The campaign-level work (did the adversary's function degrade?) has no mature method. A per-target AI share of about 0.1 today and 0.5–0.7 at the end, and a per-attack share of 0–0.1 today and 0.2–0.3 at the end, fit this evidence. These are my assumptions, not measurements.
- The slower AI path for assessment (two thirds of the end in about 10 years) fits the cyber evidence (immature methods, limits on data storage). It does not fit the kinetic evidence, where automation is already in use.
- Assessment capacity limited past campaigns (41% coverage in Desert Storm). This supports SC45's choice to make assessment a bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No public figure for analyst-hours per BDA (kinetic or cyber), or for the share of BDA that AI does at NGA or DIA.
- No source on AI use in damage assessment after offensive cyber operations.
- I did not check doctrine time standards for BDA phases (JP 3-60).

## 4. All-source intelligence analysis productivity with LLMs: official statements, studies and field experiments

### Takeaway
The one randomized experiment with military intelligence analysts (Bundeswehr University Munich, 2025) found that LLM search and summaries raised analysis scores by about 59% under time pressure. General experiments in knowledge work find about 25% faster work and better quality inside the AI's competence, but worse accuracy outside it. Official US statements describe wide use (thousands of users of a CIA tool; 20,000 Maven users) but give no measured time savings.

### Cited Findings
- **Military intelligence experiment: the AI group scored 18.21 points against 11.47 for the control group (p = 0.007) in a 30-minute analysis of 50 news articles.** Probability estimates were closer to expert judgment (absolute deviation 0.851 against 1.039, p = 0.047), and confidence did not change. The subjects were 29 active-duty soldiers in a Master of Intelligence and Security Studies program (14 with AI, 15 without). The tools were LLM search, automatic summary and named-entity recognition (Aleph Alpha). Seven experienced analysts set the benchmark; untimed, they took 3 h 49 min on average. The AI made errors on trick questions. — [Nitzl, Cyran, Krstanovic and Borghoff, Frontiers in Human Dynamics 7, 21 May 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-dynamics/articles/10.3389/fhumd.2025.1540450/full); [arXiv 2412.03610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03610). *Read: first 100,000 characters (results and limitations); Quality: experiment with a small sample of students and one text-only scenario; Maps to: output per analyst-hour ×1.59 (my calculation), which equals AI doing about 37% of the work at fixed output (1 − 1/1.59).*
- **Deloitte (2019), a task-level model from O*NET data: an all-source analyst with AI at scale could save up to 364 hours (45.5 working days) a year, 17.5% of pre-AI time.** Tasks that need social or creative intelligence get more time, not less. — [Deloitte Center for Government Insights, Mitchell, Mariani, Routh, Keyal and Mirkow, "The future of intelligence analysis"](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/insights/articles/2024/6306_future-of-intel-analysis/di-future-of-intel-analysis.pdf). *Date: 2019 (references to September 2019; hosted at a 2024 address); Read: full text, extracted locally; Quality: estimate, before LLMs; my check: 364 ÷ 0.175 = 2,080 hours, a standard work year; Maps to: an AI share of all-source work of about 0.175 at full adoption of 2019-era AI.*
- **BCG field experiment (758 consultants, GPT-4, 2023): inside the AI's competence, consultants did 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with more than 40% higher quality; outside it, they were 19 percentage points less likely to be correct.** — [Dell'Acqua et al., HBS Working Paper 24-013](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf). *Read: search summary only (one summary gives 33.9% for quality); Quality: experiment, general analytic knowledge work; Maps to: an AI share of about 0.2–0.25 on tasks inside the AI's competence in 2023, and a negative effect on ambiguous judgment tasks.*
- **Financial analysts with FactSet's generative AI (2023): 26% more distinct sources, 24% broader topics and 21% more analytical methods in reports; forecast accuracy fell where information-processing demands were high.** — [Xue, Zhang and Zhu, "Generative AI for Analysts", arXiv 2512.19705 (Dec 2025, revised Sep 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19705). *Read: abstract; Quality: natural experiment with field data; Maps to: gains in breadth of about 20–25%, with a risk to accuracy.*
- **US intelligence use is wide but not measured.** Thousands of analysts across the 18 agencies use a CIA-built generative AI (AP, May 2024). Uses include search, writing help, ideas and counterarguments (Defense One, July 2024). CIA Deputy Director Michael Ellis expects AI to be an everyday "co-worker" for analysts within a few years (April 2025). — [The Star (AP), 27 May 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/05/27/us-intelligence-agencies-embrace-of-generative-ai-is-at-once-wary-and-urgent); [Defense One, Jul 2024](https://www.defenseone.com/technology/2024/07/us-intelligence-community-embracing-generative-ai/397860/); [Washington Times, 14 Apr 2025](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/apr/14/cia-preparing-ai-agents-maintaining-human-loop/). *Read: search summaries only; Quality: official statements, reported; Maps to: the AI share of all-source work today is above zero.*
- **Cyber Command AI roadmap (2024): more than 100 activities; an AI task force in the Cyber National Mission Force runs 90-day sprints.** Gen. Timothy Haugh: "move us from opportunistic AI application to systematic adoption". Axios reports that 2026 is the first year of dedicated Cyber Command AI funding, and that its chief AI officer, Reid Novotny, is to put AI into offensive and defensive operations. — [DefenseScoop, 17 Apr 2024](https://defensescoop.com/2024/04/17/cybercom-establishes-ai-task-force/); [CyberScoop, 2 Apr 2025](https://cyberscoop.com/cyber-command-ai-gains-cybersecurity-network-monitoring/); [Axios newsletter, 2026](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-future-of-cybersecurity-b7b708a0-3e8e-11f1-b5d9-5dd7ad74133e). *Read: CyberScoop full, the others by search summary; Quality: official, reported; Maps to: the AI share of cyber operations planning starts from a low base in 2026.*
- **NSA, 2023: Haugh told the Senate that AI will not replace NSA people, and that AI outputs go to "an analyst's review".** — [USNI News, 20 Jul 2023](https://news.usni.org/2023/07/20/a-i-wont-replace-humans-at-the-nsa-nominee-tells-senate). *Read: search summary; Quality: official testimony; Maps to: human review stays.*

### Inferences
- Measured or modelled gains for analysts cluster at about 17–37% less human time for the same output: Deloitte 17.5%, BCG about 20–25%, Bundeswehr about 37% under time pressure. This supports SC45's end values of 20–30% only if "the end" means tools of 2019–2025. It does not cover agentic tools after 2026.
- Gains are larger for search, summary and triage than for judgment with ambiguous or contradictory data, where AI can lower accuracy. In SC45, target analysis is closer to the first kind of work; legal review and campaign-level assessment are closer to the second.

### Gaps
- No official US, UK or Israeli figure for analyst time saved by LLMs. A claim that DIA's ChatDIA "saved hundreds of hours" came from a weakly sourced site, and I did not use it.
- No experiment with classified data from many sources, or with experienced analysts at the pace of real work.
- RAND's report on technology and the future of Air Force intelligence analysis (RRA341-2) returned HTTP 403, so I did not use it.

## 5. Projections: what share of this work do official strategies or credible analysts expect AI to do in 5–25 years?

### Takeaway
Few sources give a number. NGA set a goal in 2017 to automate 75% of analysts' repetitive tasks. Deloitte (2019) estimated 17.5% of all-source time. McKinsey (2023) put the technical potential at 60–70% of work time for all occupations, with half of today's work automated at a midpoint of 2045. US goals are about tempo (1,000 targeting decisions an hour) and about agentic AI "from campaign planning to kill chain execution" (2026 strategy), not about shares of work.

### Cited Findings
- **NGA, 2017: Director Robert Cardillo set a goal to automate 75% of the tasks that NGA analysts do (the repetitive ones). He said manual exploitation of the commercial imagery expected over 20 years would need about 8 million imagery analysts.** — [Breaking Defense, Jun 2017](https://breakingdefense.com/2017/06/cardillo-1-million-times-more-geoint-data-in-5-years/); [Foreign Policy, 9 Jun 2017](https://foreignpolicy.com/2017/06/09/artificial-intelligence-will-put-spies-out-of-work-too/). *Date: 2017 (older); Read: search summary; Quality: official goal, and I found no later progress report; Maps to: an end AI share of about 0.75 for imagery tasks (a goal).*
- **Deloitte, 2019: 17.5% of an all-source analyst's time freed at full adoption, with no target year.** — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/insights/articles/2024/6306_future-of-intel-analysis/di-future-of-intel-analysis.pdf). *Quality: estimate.*
- **McKinsey, June 2023: generative AI and other technologies could automate work activities that absorb 60–70% of employees' time today. Half of today's work activities could be automated between 2030 and 2060, with a midpoint in 2045, eight years earlier than its previous estimate.** — [McKinsey, "The economic potential of generative AI"](https://www.mckinsey.com/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier). *Read: search summary; Quality: estimate for all occupations; Maps to: a technical ceiling of about 0.6–0.7 and an adoption midpoint of about 2045. That suggests that two thirds of the end comes later than 10 years for the whole economy.*
- **Army and CSET, 2024: "The ultimate goal is for the system and soldiers to be able to help a commander process 1,000 tactical decisions an hour."** — [Army Times](https://development.armytimes.com/?p=50904). *Quality: official goal.*
- **US AI strategy, 9 Jan 2026: "Agent Network", one of seven pace-setting projects, develops AI agents for "battle management and decision support, from campaign planning to kill chain execution". Coverage says projects are to show results within six months.** — [Inside Government Contracts, Feb 2026](https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/02/pentagon-releases-artificial-intelligence-strategy/); [copy of the memo (AFACPO)](https://www.afacpo.com/mpm/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ARTIFICIAL-INTELLIGENCE-STRATEGY-FOR-THE-DEPARTMENT-OF-WAR.pdf). *Read: search summary; Quality: official strategy; Maps to: intent to move AI into planning, not only detection.*
- **CIA, 2025: AI to be an everyday analyst "co-worker" within a few years** (Ellis). — [Washington Times, 14 Apr 2025](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/apr/14/cia-preparing-ai-agents-maintaining-human-loop/). *Read: search summary; Quality: official expectation.*
- **SCSP and ASPI, September 2024, "The Future of Intelligence Analysis: AI and Human-Machine Teaming": expects "substantial gains in the breadth and depth" of analysis over the short, medium and long term, with no numbers.** — [SCSP release](https://www.scsp.ai/2024/09/scsps-intelligence-panel-releases-joint-report-with-australian-strategic-policy-institute/). *Read: release page; Quality: expert analysis.*

### Inferences
These are my assumptions by transfer, for SC45 inputs. They are not measurements, and the ranges are not empirical intervals.
- **Planning, per target (target analysis):** today 0.1–0.3 for a leading power, because AI support is routine in kinetic targeting and less public in cyber; end 0.6–0.9 (NGA's 75% goal, the Maven and Gospel multiples, McKinsey's 60–70% ceiling); a fast path, because Maven went from tests in 2020 to routine use in 2024–26.
- **Planning, per attack (legal review, approval, coordination):** today about 0; end 0–0.2; a slow path that policy sets. A change of authorities is better modelled separately: it cut elapsed time about ×10 (a year → weeks) in 2018, and it changes few expert-years.
- **Damage assessment, per target:** today 0.05–0.2; end 0.5–0.7; a medium path.
- **Damage assessment, per attack (campaign-level effect):** today about 0; end 0.2–0.3; a slow path (the model's slower path fits).
- **Effect on the bottleneck with 30 targets per attack (my calculation).** Planning today: 1 + 0.5 × 30 = 16 expert-years per attack. At the model's end (20%): 12.8. With the split end (0.8 per target, 0.1 per attack): 0.9 + 3.0 = 3.9. Damage assessment today: 1 + 0.7 × 30 = 22; at the model's end (30%): 15.4; with the split end (0.6 per target, 0.1 per attack): 0.9 + 8.4 = 9.3. The per-target AI share is the input that matters most. Uniform shares of 20% and 30% may overstate the analyst bottleneck at the end of AI progress.
- **Cap on the per-target share.** A per-target share above about 0.9 implies human review of seconds per target. The Israeli reports tie that to a "rubber stamp" and about 10% misidentification. A model that keeps approval quality fixed should keep the per-target share below that level, or add an error cost.

### Gaps
- I found no official or think-tank projection that gives a share of targeting, legal review or damage assessment work by a given year (5–25 years ahead).
- I found no projection for offensive cyber planning specifically.
- Occupation-level exposure scores for "Intelligence Analysts" (O*NET 33-3021.06) appear only on an unverified third-party page: LLM task exposure at about the 90th percentile (Eloundou et al. method) and AI applicability at about the 48th percentile (Microsoft, Tomlinson et al. 2025). — [singulariki page](https://singulariki.ryanjhunter.workers.dev/roles/role-33-3021-06). I did not check these against the papers.
