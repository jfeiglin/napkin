# Electricity restoration during a heat wave

The power supply goes out at the start of a heat wave. The utility checks the system and crews restore service. Hospitals, cooling centres and outreach respond to the remaining need.

Residents on the affected supply, across three age bands, including home device users and people who depend on organized transport to reach cooling.

Model revision `de7af2fc41bdfb4b6e4dcba154eaa07bf3fd1eb86d066655f99e953aa6a1feb8`. Readings are instantaneous, without reporting lags. All times are in hours.

## Evidence

- **a_setting_prevalence_home_concentrators** (source_transfer). The prevalence of home concentrators and ventilators at ages 65 and over, 2,185.3 per 100,000.
- **admission** (assumption). After their battery runs out, device users still without power are admitted to hospital at 0.02 an hour on a ventilator and at a quarter of that rate on a concentrator.
- **admission_uses_place** (identity). Each admission uses one place; the model moves the person and the place together.
- **all_powered** (assumption). In one comparison chosen for this scenario, every cooled place has its own backup power, so every place can open during the blackout.
- **backup_protected** (assumption). People with backup cooling are protected from the start of the outage through the heat wave.
- **by_response** (assumption). Outreach starts when the cooled places open in each defender response, at hour 4, 18 or 36. It acts only while the power is still off.
- **clinical_share_limits** (assumption). National studies of emergency visits give the three clinical shares. The scenario chooses a range around each share to cover other settings. These ranges are not measured intervals.
- **contact** (assumption). Responders find device users from the lists that public health authorities can request in an emergency, and take half of those without power and not seriously ill to sites with power.
- **conversion** (source_transfer). The method converts the felt temperature into an equivalent outdoor temperature. It adds the rise in the felt temperature, divided by the group's slope, to the outdoor temperature.
- **cooling_centre_capacity** (source_transfer). The authors of the method judge that cooling centres hold no more than 1 to 2% of a city's people in a concurrent heat wave and blackout.
- **cost_chosen** (assumption). This cost is chosen for this scenario. It applies to the people in the study area, in US dollars at 2025 prices.
- **counts** (assumption). The supply serves 24,000 residential and 3,000 non-residential customers, chosen for a supply of 60,000 people.
- **counts_delivered** (identity). The model counts what the response actually delivers.
- **counts_extra_support** (identity). The model counts the extra support that each group of people uses.
- **counts_group_people** (identity). The model counts each group of people and each resource separately and once. Each value and each transfer keeps its own source.
- **counts_quantity_value** (identity). The model counts this quantity. Each value that changes it has its own source.
- **counts_work_done** (identity). The model counts the work done and the work still to do.
- **curve_limits** (assumption). The scenario chooses the limits of the four all-cause curves for a reader to explore. They are not measured intervals. The limits of each value include the values of all four studies that the method's authors list. By default, the scenario gives all the weight to the curve of Gasparrini et al. 2015.
- **curve_shape** (source_transfer). Each curve's log risk ratio rises in a straight line from its threshold to the top of its fitted range, and continues above the top with the slope of its last degree, as in the method's code.
- **curve_table** (source). Table S1 of the method's 2026 supplement gives each study's threshold and the top of its fitted range for the setting.
- **curve_weights** (assumption). The all-cause curve is the weighted mean of the four curves' log risk ratios. The method weights each study by the inverse variance of its slope, which is not published; the Phoenix risk ratios that the method reports fit only a pool led by the Gasparrini curve, so that curve has all the weight by default.
- **dark_calibration** (assumption). The rise in full darkness makes the felt temperature, averaged over the source's 48 hours of full blackout and an even return of power over the next 72 hours, equal the source's mean without power. With the default heat-up time, the felt temperature in full darkness is 35.7 °C (evidence/iet-dark-calibration.md).
- **ed_normal_rate** (source_transfer). The method's normal rate of 5.9 heat-related emergency visits a day in its Phoenix population of 1.39 million (arithmetic).
- **exposure_weighted** (assumption). Person-hours at home without effective cooling during the heat wave, weighted by the share of the supply still off.
- **families_see_need** (assumption). Families see whether their power is back and whether the heat goes on. They leave only while both last and the time for private departures has not ended.
- **felt_temperature_limits** (assumption). The scenario chooses the limits of the temperatures that people feel, with power and in the source's blackout, for a reader to explore. They are not measured intervals. The limits include the source's Phoenix values: 26.3 °C with power and 32.7 °C in the blackout.
- **fit_range** (source_transfer). The cost fit runs through the calculator's 2-hour and 24-hour values. No customer is back within 2 hours, because switching waits for the checks.
- **gasparrini_slope_arithmetic** (source_transfer). The risk ratio of 1.21 at 40.6 °C against 33.3 °C, spread evenly over the log risk ratio: ln 1.21 / 7.3 (arithmetic).
- **hazus_severity** (convention). Serious casualties are the people who die and the people who survive with serious harm that lasts: the Hazus severity levels S4 and S3.
- **heat_curve_limits** (assumption). The scenario chooses these limits for a reader to explore. They are not measured intervals. They apply to the curve of heat-related deaths and to the curve of heat emergency visits, which the scenario's method of heat risk uses. The limits of each value include the value that the scenario takes from studies of Phoenix.
- **heat_death_normal_rate** (source_transfer). The method's normal rate of 0.07 heat-related deaths a day in its Phoenix population of 1.39 million (arithmetic). It leaves out the deaths of extreme heat, which the observed yearly rates include.
- **heat_deaths_counted** (assumption). The model counts deaths and heatstroke survivors in each group, each person once.
- **heat_excess** (assumption). For people at home without power during the heat wave, the outage adds deaths at the larger of two rates, from the outdoor to the equivalent outdoor temperature. One is the age band's normal death rate times the rise in the all-cause risk ratio; the other is the normal rate of heat-related deaths times the rise in their risk ratio, up to the cap. Heatstroke follows the same rule from the normal rate of heat emergency visits, and comes only from the first visit of each person. People with backup cooling or power, in cooled places, moved away or in hospital keep the temperature that they feel with power. Most indoor heat deaths in Maricopa County in 2024 were in homes whose air conditioning did not work. The pattern held in 2025. Deaths in New York City rose on the days of the 2003 citywide outage. Localized outages in New York City were studied as well.
- **heat_up** (assumption). Without power, the felt temperature approaches its value in full darkness with the heat-up time. The outage starts at the start of the run, so the time without power is the time since the start.
- **heat_wave_frame** (assumption). The heat wave starts with the outage and lasts as long as its chosen length. The outage adds heat risk only while the heat wave lasts.
- **heat_wave_outdoor** (source_transfer). The mean of the five daily mean temperatures at Phoenix airport from 20 to 24 July 2006 (38.05, 39.75, 40.85, 39.2 and 38.9 °C).
- **integrates_delivered** (identity). Integrates the support actually delivered.
- **keeps_exact_count** (identity). The model keeps an exact count of this quantity.
- **national_rates** (source_transfer). The national death rate of the age band in 2023, the WHO rates of its single years of age weighted by the census counts (arithmetic; evidence/age-band-rates.md).
- **occupants_keep_place** (assumption). People in cooled places keep their place until the power returns or the heat ends, then go home, where they are protected.
- **officials_see_need** (assumption). Officials learn from local reports and from the weather whether the power is still off and the heat goes on. They open the cooled places only while both last; when the power returns or the heat ends as the places become ready, they stand the response down.
- **one_person_per_visit** (assumption). Each heatstroke visit counts as one person. The range tests the effect of repeat visits.
- **open_by_response** (assumption). The scenario chooses when cooled places open in each defender response. They open at hour 4 in the early response, at hour 18 in the standard response and at hour 36 in the delayed response.
- **order_of_work** (assumption). Crews clear extra inspection work first, then repeat work, then ordinary restoration.
- **own_power** (source_transfer). A quarter of device users have another power supply that runs their device through the outage; the others depend on their battery.
- **people_held** (assumption). People held inside the compound, at the scale reported for one large compound. The people in the homes are the rest of the people on the supply.
- **people_on_supply** (assumption). Everyone on the supply: the people in the compound and in the homes around it.
- **person_only_health** (identity). Each person is in only one health group, so the model counts each person once. Deaths count once within serious casualties.
- **places_with_power** (assumption). The places are inside the outage, so only those with their own power can open. Each place holds one person at a time.
- **power_law** (source_transfer). A customer's cost is its 24-hour cost times the outage hours divided by 24 to the fitted power. Each customer counts the cost of its own outage when its circuit comes back; customers still without power count the cost so far. The calculator was not designed for outages over 24 hours, so the longer part is a convention.
- **private_departures_end** (assumption). Private departures end with their window, or earlier once the need has ended.
- **private_movers_leave** (assumption). A tenth of the people in homes without backup move away by their own means, at an even rate between hours 4 and 28, to places outside the official plan. People who die before their departure do not move.
- **records_status_events** (identity). The model records this status as events happen.
- **recovery_inputs** (assumption). The outage observation, safety holds, and work orders are supplied by the model user. They are not forecasts.
- **restoration_switching** (assumption). The hours that the standard crews need to switch every circuit back on when the equipment is intact and no checks are needed. Observed restorations after blackouts with intact plant took 6 to 30 hours; the scenario chooses 15.
- **service_follows_work** (assumption). Circuits come back one by one as crews do the switching work, so the share of people with power is the share of the switching work done. A circuit that is back stays back; extra work affects only circuits still awaiting restoration. While the supply is off, crews have not started to switch, so no circuit is back.
- **setting_groups** (assumption). The people in the compound, the device users and the other people in the homes are separate groups, and each group is split into three age bands. Together they add up to the people on the supply, and each person is in one group and one band.
- **severe_as_intensive_care** (assumption). The scenario assumes that a tenth of the heatstroke survivors are as severe as the intensive-care patients of the 1995 Chicago heat wave.
- **share_by_waiting** (assumption). Officials take people who are not seriously ill to the places, sharing the intake among the groups in proportion to the people who wait in each group at the start. The compound's people count only once officials open its gates.
- **switching_starts** (assumption). Crews wait for isolation, assessment and safety checks before restoring service.
- **visits_at_home** (assumption). The heat emergency visits that each person at home has so far, at the normal rate of visits times the rise in their risk ratio, while the heat wave lasts and the power is off.
- **work_cost** (assumption). The standard crews are 40 lineworkers. A lineworker-hour costs twice the median wage, for benefits, overtime, vehicles and equipment. The cost counts the switching work actually done and the additional work recorded by the utility.
