# IV-fluid supply and recovery

A manufacturer pauses IV-fluid shipments. Hospital reserves, procurement and outside supply cover demand while the release team qualifies its systems and reviews lots. The model follows fluid, work, patient courses and costs for sixty days.

One hypothetical manufacturer and a network of 100 hospitals.

Model revision `c2db483bb79cbd471dc75cb3300a77441d780cfc613a92d3a4cfcbfe8d4f5a2d`. Readings are instantaneous, without reporting lags. All times are in hours.

## Evidence

- **a_consequences_2026_three_inputs_turn** (assumption). Three inputs turn missed complete courses into harm. They are the share with severe dehydration chosen for this scenario, the stated rescue capacity and the source's difference in risk with and without rehydration. Deaths count only the risk above that with timely rehydration.
- **a_pilot_assumed_brief** (assumption). Assumed for this scenario, from the scenario brief.
- **balances_fluid_stocks** (identity). The model balances the fluid stocks or counts each patient in one group only. It uses the stated assumptions of this pilot scenario.
- **balances_stocks_work** (identity). The model balances stocks, work done and transfers, so that no quantity is created or lost.
- **card_text_pilot** (assumption). This text describes, in plain words, the event that the model calculates.
- **counts_fixed_group** (identity). The model counts a fixed group of people and gives all costs at one price year. It uses the inputs recorded for this scenario.
- **distinct_people** (assumption). Each unique person has one complete urgent course, so the deaths and the serious survivors are different people.
- **hazus_severity** (convention). Serious casualties are the people who die and the people who survive with serious harm that lasts: the Hazus severity levels S4 and S3.
- **high_risk_patient** (identity). Each high-risk patient who misses a complete course goes into one of two groups. Rescue comes first, up to its daily capacity, and the other patients are not rescued.
- **keeps_count_limited** (identity). The model keeps a count of each limited stock of physical items. Items move between stocks and work adds up over time, but no item is created or lost.
- **pace** (assumption). How fast the manufacturer restores its systems, requalifies release and reconciles its records. The pace is twice the standard pace, the standard pace and 60% of it in the early, standard and limited responses. At the standard pace the ordinary work takes three weeks. One work-hour an hour is one work-hour of progress each hour: the work of one full-time team on this task.
- **product_reconciliation** (assumption). When a product check finds a discrepancy, affected lots are held until the records are reconciled against the product.
- **qualification_release_come** (source_transfer). Qualification and release come before use. The fixed 24-hour delivery time is an assumption of this pilot scenario.
- **qualification_work** (assumption). The team completes ordinary qualification work first and then processes pending records review at its declared pace.
- **rt_sector_2026_assumes_l_day** (assumption). The scenario assumes 600 L/day. Published incidents show how this works, but only under the conditions that they observed. The range of values used to test this input is also an assumption.
- **service_recovery** (assumption). A disruption pauses qualified shipments. The response assumptions determine the time to contain the incident, restore the records service and procure fluid.
