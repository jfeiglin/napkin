# Brief A3: solver numerics

Folder: `scratch/rewrite/a3/`. Read `briefs/COMMON.md` first.

## Question

Which algorithms compute the selection order of the spec, and what do they cost at 30,000 places?
The answer decides the design of the solve library (`napkin/engine/solve/`), which will serve both
the sharing of endpoints and the declared solvers.

Earlier measurements, for orientation (30,000 places, 35,000 routes, 15,000 sources): a cold linear
program 1.2 s in the substituted form and 2.9 s with two one-way flows for each route; warm 40 to
200 ms; each further ranked objective about 1.7 s; the distance step 0.1 s with no shortage and 0.4
to 2.7 s with shortage; the QP of HiGHS itself 73 s at one tenth of the scale.

## Method

Standalone, on sparse arrays, in `solve/`.

1. **One problem form**: unknowns with bounds; affine `==`, `<=` and `>=` rows; ranked linear
   objectives (a positive-part shortfall is an extra unknown); then the max-min served fractions of
   each priority tier; then the squared distance of the flows to their requests; then that of the
   other unknowns to their reference values, each with its scale.
2. **Ranked objectives on one kept instance.** How is an attained optimum kept: a new row
   `c.x <= optimum + tolerance`, or bounds fixed from the reduced costs? Compare for correctness
   and cost. Choose the tolerance.
3. **The fair-sharing rank at scale**, on the route-and-level network (signed routes with a level
   at each place). Raise the common fraction; find the claims that are fixed at that level from the
   duals, not with one program for each member; fix them; go on. Measure the number of levels and
   the time with no shortage, some shortage, a damaged network, and several priority tiers. On
   small random networks, check the answers against brute force with one program for each member.
4. **The squared-distance step with `<=` and `>=` rows.** Extend `nearest.py` or replace it. Mend
   the stop rule. Check: `4 <= x + y <= 6` with targets (0, 0) gives (2, 2), and with targets
   (5, 5) gives (3, 3); `x + y == 1` with targets zero gives (1/2, 1/2); the levels of the worked
   network; random small problems against an exact calculation over each set of active rows. Then
   measure at scale.
5. **Unknowns that join and leave.** Does the HiGHS binding that SciPy bundles let you add and
   delete columns and rows on a kept instance? Does the warm start survive? Compare with a problem
   that is formed again. Check the membership case: a 3 L/h limit and 2 L/h for each occurrence
   give 2; then 1.5 each; then 2; then nothing. At each moment the answer equals that of a problem
   formed fresh.
6. **Another spelling of the units.** Scale some columns (W to kW). The answer must not change
   beyond the tolerance, and neither may the claims that are fixed at each level. The worked network
   in kW gives 0.004 kW.
7. **The same result again.** One problem solved cold, solved warm after another problem, and
   solved in another place of a batch: is the published answer the same within the tolerance? If
   not, say where it differs and why. The design rule is that a kept instance belongs to one run.
8. **A small solve inside an integration.** How long does one solve of about ten unknowns take?
   How many can run each second? Then read `Allocation.ration` and say which of its closed forms
   stay right as fast paths under the new rules: a request at the flow, at the source and at the
   sink; priority tiers with ties; a flow with legs where one leg asks nothing (today an error,
   `allocation.py:165`; allowed by the spec).
9. **Tolerances**: for the ranked optima, and for an ordinary formula that reads an equality which
   the solver enforced.

## Expected answers

(2, 2) and (3, 3). (1/2, 1/2). 2, then 1.5 each, then 2. (2, 2, 3) and (4, 0, 5). The worked
network: rates 4 and 4 with supply 8; clinic first 6 and 2; at 1 h 3 and 3; at 2 h 6 and 0; levels
(8/3, -4/3, -4/3) and (3, -3, 0). Staffing 70 and 30, then 56 and 24. The pump: 2, 2 and 0. The
provider: 6 and 2.

## Rulings that you owe

The algorithm for each rank. The tolerances. A go or a no-go for each of three risks: fair sharing
at scale, inequality rows in the distance step, and membership that changes. Where the answer is a
no-go, the fallback may be a slower exact method, or a refusal by name above a size that you
measured. It may never give another answer: a change in who is served is a change to the language,
and you report it as one for the owner. The zero reference of a solver-local unknown. The fast
paths of `allocation.py` that stay.
