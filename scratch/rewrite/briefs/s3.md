## The subset of S3

From `spec.md` ("Flows and algebraic solvers", "Shared resources and competing invocations") and the
part "Keep the three flow forms distinct" of `g0-decisions.md`:
- **A reversible route** `flow link: west.water <-> east.water` has one signed `actual_rate`, a
  capacity on its magnitude and a signed request. Its legs `link.forward` and `link.backward` are
  members of the endpoint collections, each reading the positive part of the rate in its direction
  (-3 L/h reads 0 forward and 3 backward). `priority(...)` names a leg; a bare reversible flow there
  is an error. Both ends must be stores that can supply and receive: `SOURCE`, `SINK` and a tally are
  refused. Decide, and say in your last message, how a store is known to be a tally (a store that
  only receives) when the language has no mark for one.
- **A flow with several legs**: a parent with nested `flow leg: a -> b` lines, each with its own
  request and capacity, and one `served_fraction` from 0 to 1. At fraction f each leg carries f times
  its request, each in its own unit (legs of 4 L/h and 8 Wh/h at half service carry 2 L/h and 4 Wh/h).
  A leg that requests zero carries zero; legs of 0 and 8 with ample capacity give 0 and 8 at fraction 1
  (today an error); legs of 0 and 0 give fraction 0. The parent has no `request` or `actual_rate`. A
  leg is named in `priority(...)` at its own endpoint. Legs that two endpoints rank differently: a
  claim waits for every claim that any endpoint ranks ahead of it (the oracle's rule, R-A2-12).
- **Reads of a flow's ends** (`.source`, `.sink`), which S2 refused: give them a type, or keep them
  refused with a reason; say which.
- Events (S4) and solvers (S5) stay refused.
