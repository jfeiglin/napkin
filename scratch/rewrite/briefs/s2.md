## The subset of S2

From `spec.md`, "Flows and algebraic solvers" up to "An entity owns its solver", and the
decisions on flows in `g0-decisions.md`:
- **Endpoints.** A store that supplies a flow gains `outflow`, one that receives gains `inflow`. Each
  has `capacity` (hard), `request` (soft) and `allocation`: `proportional()` by default, or
  `priority(...)` over named incident flows or a projection (`customers.by(rank).delivery`), lower
  ranks first, equal ranks sharing in proportion, unlisted flows sharing the rest. `.flows` is the
  collection of the incident flows, `.actual_rate` their sum.
- **Flows across templates and entities**: ends through links (`this.origin.power`) and at named
  entities (`reservoir.water`); `SOURCE` and `SINK`; a `[0, 0]` store is a junction whose inflow
  equals its outflow at each instant.
- **Local sharing by default** (G0): each endpoint shares among its own flows by its own rule, also
  when junctions and full or empty stores make rates depend on one another. The two-junction
  example: 1/2, 1/4 and 1/4 L/h. Fair sharing across endpoints happens only in a declared solver
  (S5). Two outflows of one store with no allocation written share in proportion (today a compile
  error).
- **Requests are soft, capacities hard** (D15); a store at its lower bound passes on what flows in, a
  store at its upper bound passes on the room freed by what flows out.
- **Moving rates at any size** (D3): a request or a capacity that reads a moving quantity is valid
  in a connected set of any size. A large set runs on a sparse integrator (the measurement of
  `scratch/rewrite/a4a/FINDINGS.md`, T2.3: 60,000 stores to 48 h in 32 s while no store rests at a
  bound). Size never changes the equations; a model may stop with an explicit failure of time,
  memory or accuracy.
- Reversible flows and flows with legs stay refused (S3), and so do events (S4) and solvers (S5).

## S2-E: the engine

You own what S1-E owned. Run the subset above:
- **One sparse network** across the pools: the incidence of flows and endpoints, the totals of each
  endpoint, and local sharing at each endpoint, settled in the direction of supply (the oracle's
  rule, `tests/reference/network.py`). Reuse the closed forms of `napkin/engine/allocation.py` that
  `scratch/rewrite/a3/FINDINGS.md` (R-A3-09) confirmed, for a proportional group alone and a
  priority order with no ties. Where local sharing has no defined answer that you can compute (the
  oracle raises `NotCovered` for room passed upstream through two full stores and for a ring of empty
  stores), stop with a diagnostic that names the stores, never with another answer.
- **The sparse integrator** for a large connected set whose rates move. Replace the dense matrix of
  `napkin/engine/continuous/` for such a set; one evaluation of the rates touches only the nonzeros.
- **The carry-over of S1**: a failure inside a long numerical segment is timed where it happens, not
  at the segment's start; per-row formulas of a large table are not fitted row by row.
- **Tests**: every example of the subset against its expected results and the oracle; a generator
  of small random networks (a few stores, junctions, priorities, capacities and requests), compared
  with the oracle: a few hundred cases in a normal run, more under `deep`. Port the engine tests of
  slice S2 in `scratch/rewrite/tests-triage.csv` that the subset covers, keeping their closed forms;
  `tests/engine/test_rationing.py` is rewritten for the new rules (class `RULES`). List each file.
- **Benchmark**, once at the end: the 30,000-place network of A4a with routes as flows between place
  rows, first with rates that change only at moments, then with requests that read a stock. Give the
  times in your last message.
