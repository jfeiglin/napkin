# One affine crossing exhausts a 512-evaluation budget

The original macOS deep tests for seed 1900 failed in Component and Sparse at code revision `7eb6dae75bf9c4816279e299b60463b190206f03`. An isolated export of that revision reproduced both failures. See [pytest output](seed1900.log) and [JUnit](seed1900.xml).

The [Component model](component.scn) and [Sparse model](sparse.scn) have two stocks. Level grows as 6 + 2.4t. Its comparison target grows as 5.25 + 3.6t. Their difference 0.75 − 1.2t has one crossing at 5/8 h. The meter credits delivery while the level is at least the target. The comparison band changes its continuous integral by less than the generator's 2e-8 L allowance. The exact reference reports delivery 1/4, 5/8 and 5/8 L at 1/4, 3/4 and 1 h. A capacity declaration selects Sparse and permits the entire drive rate.

At budget 512, Component stops at 0.6249999999999994 h and Sparse at 0.624999999999999 h. At budget 4096, both locate one physical crossing and reach 1 h with 0.625 L to the displayed precision. The [Component profile](probe-component.log) and [Sparse profile](probe-sparse.log) show where the budget went. At contact, the departure probe has length 1.4901161193847656e-8 h. Level's rate is 2.4 L/h at both the contact and its midpoint. The downstream delivery rate changes from 1 to 0. The all-coordinate displacement test in `napkin/engine/continuous/integrate.py:863` sees that change and starts the adaptive integration at line 867. Fitting the delivery-rate jump uses 541 field evaluations in each method. Neither operand of the comparison reads delivery.

The generator's 2,000 seed specifications include 33 inclusive crossings with an initially positive difference and negative relative slope. Seed 1420 matches the slopes and crossing time of 1900, with a different starting level. These metadata matches identify related shapes, without predicting specific pass/fail outcomes. Seed 1900's exact parameter tuple occurs once. The primary deep output also logged failures under other switch shapes.

`replay.py` runs a minimized model at the requested budget and reports the actual handoff and exact reference result. `profile.py` adds call counts around the outgoing increment integration. Both instruments preserve the engine's returned values.
