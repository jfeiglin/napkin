# Physical stock accounting cutover

This inventory describes production code at `97d7ae5`. The cross-method pass-through tests landed in `5f02043`. The transfer prototypes measure accepted amounts on some methods. At this revision, stage reads, range checks and restarts still obtain stock levels from several places. The proposed changes below depend on the performance gate.

## Clock and physical balances

A physical leg has one concrete connection ID and two optional physical stock IDs. `SOURCE` and `SINK` supply the missing endpoints. Book the same accepted leg amount as an outflow and an inflow where those endpoints exist. Resolve route legs individually before applying their incidence to stocks. The aggregate route, endpoint totals and served fraction each describe rates already represented by those connections. Posting any of those again duplicates mass. A self-loop has zero net change and positive gross amounts in both directions.

A movable, active store at a lower, upper or coincident bound permits balanced pass-through if both gross directions are feasible. Check net bound feasibility on the simultaneous batch. Sequential debit-first or credit-first checks reject legitimate pass-through. Report the earliest frozen violation before committing later movement. ## Numerical method handoff

| Method at the reviewed commit | Live producer and remaining work |
| --- | --- |
| `Family`, `Lines` | Both expose `transferred(connection, t)` using `HeldAmounts`. Rate changes and retirement close the prior rectangle. Preserve batched held work and post only newly accepted `rate × elapsed` per physical connection. A held row can start before the current global moment. |
| `ComponentWay` | `_commit` accepts the amount before `component.proceed`; `_transfer_amount` uses a direct polynomial primitive, affine/exact solution, isolated incidence row or Radau collocation. `transferred` can read an accepted prefix before or after closing it. `step`, `snap`, `cut` and `retire` meet the same installation and must agree on its cursor. |
| `Sparse` | `_accept`, `step`, `snap`, `cut` maintain accepted dense increments. `_transfer_amount` can use a clock primitive or `radau_amount`. `leg_rows` identifies physical connections even when one sparse instance contains several sets. Use stable connection IDs when sets regroup; a solver trial or finite-difference Jacobian evaluation supplies no transfer of its own. |
| `DeclaredWay` | `_settled`, `_publish`, `_through` and `_lines` get a solved or locally shared rate, then build store slopes. The reviewed revision has no `transferred` implementation here. Close the previous held allocation on a new solve, with physical-leg rates after route direction and local sharing have been resolved. Preserve the solver basis and result publication. |
| `MovingDeclared` | `_at`, `_net`, `_derivative` and `_commit` evolve net inventory, and `_pieces` fits recorder output. The reviewed revision has no moving-leg transfer API here. Supply each accepted physical leg from the installed allocation and clip it at the accepted end. A net derivative cannot distinguish a balanced cycle from idle pipes. A recorder fit also changes with requested history. |

`MovingAmounts.total/remainder/through` are method-local cumulative amounts since installation. They do not identify a stock's all-time balance or prove what another owner already posted. Maintain that distinction across an internal step and a mid-step stage. In particular, subtracting two large cumulative floats merely to recover a small accepted increment can lose a transfer before it reaches the stock. Prefer the method's newly accepted increment or its exact/compensated difference. Stop at a captured scalar, frozen formula, arithmetic operation or cycle. `batch + 0 L`, `batch - 1 L` and earlier scalar captures use ordinary amount rules. For a whole-stock move, read its full balance including prior same-stage additions. | `Resolver.assignment`, `program.check`, `Moment.resolved`, `target`, `Plan._keyed`, `_referred`, `project` | `Moment.resolved` caches a path description; a concrete occurrence selected by a live lookup belongs to the current statement. ## Checkpoints and inspection

`Breaks.apply` puts a statement-stop edit into the replayed stage and commits a stage-stop edit immediately. `Breaks.transferred` currently records `float(new) - float(old)` in the external book. Use the authoritative old and replacement balances for the edit and book their difference once on commit. Preserve `Breaks.checked` range, owner and type validation and its setup/builtin restrictions.

`saved.at_stop` uses a shallow copy of `Run` and calls `projected.finish_arrival()` to prepare checkpoint data. A mutable balance or cursor shared with the live run would double-post at inspection. Fork the book for this projection or make the preview pure. `saved.save`, `_run`, `_instance`, `_made`, `_restore_ways`, `_occurrences` and `Run.finish_arrival` must agree whether the prefix was booked before the pause. Serialize concrete balances, carried precision, posting times, cumulative frozen directions and any necessary method origin or pending prefix. Restore them before a replayed stage reads or edits stock. `opening_levels`, `move_roundoff` and the old reconstructed method arrays are present at this commit. Retire their independent accounting authority only when pause-before-statement and pause-before-stage both pass live, serialized and fresh-process checks. Engine code identity already participates in checkpoint verification. [Checkpoint edits](../../../napkin/engine/timeline/checkpoints.py) · [Saved run state](../../../napkin/engine/timeline/saved.py)
