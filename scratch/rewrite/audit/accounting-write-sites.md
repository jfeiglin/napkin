## Trace a delivery

The refund example starts with 1 L in `plant.donor` and 10,000,000 L in `plant.pool`. The proposed transfer has a time-dependent rate and a stage can interrupt it. The following trace uses the same physical connection all the way to the reported balance. [Executable trace on Component and Sparse](../../../tests/engine/test_refund_prefix.py)

A concrete leg can have two physical endpoints, one endpoint at `SOURCE` or `SINK`, or the same physical store at both ends. Route totals, served fractions and endpoint rate reports describe existing connections. Only the concrete connection supplies a transferred amount. Each endpoint consumes its outstanding signed incidence on demand. [Physical stock writer](../../../napkin/engine/timeline/stocks.py) · [Method contract](../../../napkin/engine/timeline/way.py)

## Cost of a moment

`Stocks` holds a balance, consumed incidence, and last posting time for each materialized store. Repeated reads at the same accepted time reuse that balance. Reads can project a balance without advancing its cursor. Held transfers accumulate locally since the last rate change. Moving methods maintain their numerical prefixes. A local stage or group rebuild closes affected stocks, and a checkpoint or end-of-run projection can inspect the requested cutoff. The recorder and published endpoint totals do not debit or credit a stock.

In the 96-store test a quarter-hour empty event advances zero posting cursors. At the half-hour move only `tank0.water` and `bank.received` advance, on both Family and Lines. Reversing the order of subsequent stock reads causes zero further postings. The benchmark records actual cursor advances separately from attempted postings and numerical primitive batches. ## Follow a grouped contact

Two inlets deliver 0.5 and 1.5 L/h and an outlet removes 1 L/h. The tank reaches 2 L at 1 h. If a scheduled stage falls 10⁻¹⁰ h earlier, the timeline puts both causes at the stage time. The stock writer closes the remaining 10⁻¹⁰ L on the two inlets in shares of one quarter and three quarters. Each inlet's source pays its share. The outlet keeps its accepted rectangle. With a stage equally far past 1 h, the same two ledgers give the excess back. The tests check each leg, all four balances and exact conservation in the independent rational interpreter. [Grouped threshold and bound tests](../../../tests/engine/test_threshold_transfer_placement.py)

One corrected leg can touch a second stock already on its own threshold or resting on its bound. The stock writer includes that neighbor in the same incidence equations. For two outlets sharing 1 L/h at an empty store, their recorded amounts sum exactly to the inlet's amount and the store stays at zero. A stage that touches a different component does no work on their books. A checkpoint saves the numerical opening level used for a single draining donor alongside its accepted amount, so resuming gives the same physical balance. When a reader leaves a shared moving integration, the remaining producers reopen from their accepted balances and keep flowing. [Balanced and lazy incidence](../../../tests/engine/test_resting_stock_incidence.py) · [Moving group membership](../../../tests/engine/test_moving_reads.py)

For a closed pipe from A to B, A starts at 4 L and B at zero. At the 1 h stage, A's target of 3 L and B's target of 0.9999999999 L would require different transfers on the pipe. The timeline keeps both contacts at 1 h. The stock writer keeps the trajectory's 1 L transfer, giving A = 3 L and B = 1 L. Each stock lies within its own band. The recorder retains both targets and measured gaps as a near-tie. When B's target is 0.999999995 L, its gap exceeds its band. The run fails at 1 h and retains its incoming balances, including the 2 L already received by a separate store. It posts none of that moment's proposed corrections. The controls exercise both endpoints of one pipe and two targets on one endpoint. [Transfer and rollback comparisons](../../../tests/engine/test_threshold_transfer_placement.py) · [Public source and artifact reports](../../../tests/commands/test_run_report.py)

A finished donor has an allowance of 1 L and its accepted outflow at 1 h has used that litre. The preferred placement would add 10⁻¹⁰ L. The receiver is within its own band, so its watch fires while the donor keeps 99 L, the receiver keeps 1 L and the frozen outflow remains exactly 1 L. An independent receiver can reach its feasible 1.9999999999 L target. With a 5 × 10⁻⁹ L gap, the receiver is outside its band. Under amended change 27, the 1 h horizon arrives before that receiver’s contact. Its stock and gross outflow stay at 1 L. The independent receiver can still take its eligible placement. A longer run that actually crosses a frozen allowance reports all contributing legs. The same test family covers zero and positive source floors and a destination with insufficient room. A stage 10⁻¹⁰ h before its empty contact leaves about 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ L on A's books. Moving that last amount would spoil B's placement. The timeline retains the grouped contact and accepted transfers. At the next allocation A's own band closes its outflow. B continues supplying 1.5 L/h from its balance. ## Numerical coordinate writers and physical posting

For a contact moved to a nearby moment, `ContactInterval` uses the part of the incoming trajectory which satisfies both grouping tests. A distinct earlier stage keeps its accepted stock reading. Each method supplies its existing bounds on motion to certify that the stock stayed inside its band over that part. A stock can leave and re-enter the band without crossing its threshold. Only the final connected portion can fund the placement. If the method cannot certify sufficient hard-rate headroom, the books retain their accepted amounts and the trace identifies the constraint. A correction applies at the physical moment. Two dense samples taken arbitrarily close on opposite sides can therefore differ by the in-band correction as well as the intervening flow. Dividing that difference by the sample spacing does not recover the pipe’s published rate. [Cross-method capacity and checkpoint controls](../../../tests/engine/test_capacity_headroom_history.py) · [Numerical and exact turning-stock controls](../../../tests/engine/test_moving_placement_capacity.py)

At `c974810` a method could install a located target independently of the postings that funded it. The following inventory names the old writer in each path, its replacement, and where the remaining numerical operations matter. The timeline passes accepted targets through `Stocks.place`. Its posting decision uses the incoming physical amounts and can retain a residual. The method receives the resulting coordinate for its next trajectory. ### Expectation changes from the stock-placement ruling

The following table records the cutover at `4ed5afa`. The later amendment to change 27 supersedes its time-only grouping expectations. The present rule checks both distances before considering a placement. See the updated ledger for each early/late stage, horizon, cap and allowance case. [Amended grouping expectations](comparison-expectations-0.2.3.md#time-and-stock-distance-at-a-contact)

| Fixture | Previous expectation | New observation and control |
|---|---|---|
| Finished donor, grouped threshold | A 10⁻¹⁰ L positive placement increment failed as a frozen excess. | At 1 h both gross and receiver stay at 1 L, donor at 99 L. The grouped triggers run. With a 5 × 10⁻⁹ L gap the placement fails and retains its attempted increment and 1 L allowance in the diagnostic. A real gross crossing during the incoming interval still fails at its located time. |
| Stock already empty, two idle legs | The first declared idle leg received the 10⁻¹⁸ L residual. | Both ledgers stay unchanged and the donor retains the residual. The adjacent 1:2 positive-rate control still spends the entire residual in those proportions. |
| Hard 1 L/h declared inflow, upper bound in a scheduled stage’s time window | A stage at 1 h could see 1.000000005 L. The breakpoint similarly stored the future bound. | An outside-band 5 × 10⁻⁹ L shortage fails before the stage and preserves the delivered amount. For a 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ L shortage the stage and checkpoint see the physically delivered amount, approximately 1 L, and allocation treats the tank as full. Withdrawing to 0.25 L at the stop reopens the pipe. A soft pipe can still make a feasible placement. |
| Other endpoint lacks room at its floor or ceiling | The original 5 × 10⁻⁹ L outside-band case failed. | Retain that failure. With a 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ L gap, the donor’s positive floor or the receiver’s ceiling blocks the preferred change. The main pair keeps its accepted gross and balances, an independent group finishes, and the firings keep their authored order. |
| Independent clock switch and almost simultaneous tank bound | Both the uncoupled and the dynamically capped supply variants previously reported 1.0000076293945312 L at 1 h, although the tank pipe had a hard 1 L/h limit. | The 2⁻¹⁷ L shortage exceeds the tank’s roughly 10⁻⁹ L band. Both variants now keep the accepted litre and fail before the stage. Removing the tank pipe’s cap leaves a soft request whose compatible placement retains both original contacts. |
| Hard pipe or shared endpoint cap | New test. | Two legs carry ¼ and ¾ L. A preferred increase would exceed their individual caps or the shared 1 L/h cap. The inside-band run retains the directed amounts and both counterpart balances. The outside-band run retains the incoming prefix and reports the full target list. A separate fixture rejects two disjoint caps together, inspects both gross amounts, and verifies that their stock names appear in sorted order despite their reversed declaration order. |
