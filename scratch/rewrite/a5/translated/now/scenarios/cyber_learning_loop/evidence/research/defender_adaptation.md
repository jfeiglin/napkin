## 1. Specific adaptation: after a flaw or tool is disclosed or used publicly, how fast do patching, signatures and detection rise?

### Takeaway
Specific adaptation is fast at first, then it stalls. For famous internet-facing flaws, 70–92% of the exposed hosts were fixed within 3–4 weeks (half-life about 1–2 weeks), but a residual of about 5–30% stayed open for months or years. In large enterprise datasets the median known-exploited flaw takes 1–6 months to fix, and only 26–54% are fully fixed within the year. Other attackers exploit a disclosed flaw within days. A patch often closes only the exploited path, and indicators spread poorly between defenders. The specific part therefore closes one door for most targets, but not for all, and it does not reach other doors.

### Inferences
- The specific part has two phases. My calculation, exponential-equivalent rates in the fast phase: Heartbleed, 73% of the hosts vulnerable at day 2 fixed within a month, gives about 15 per year (half-life about 16 days); ProxyLogon, 82,000 to about 30,000 unpatched servers in 11 days, gives about 0.09 per day or 33 per year (half-life about 7.6 days). Typical enterprise KEVs are much slower: the Bitsight median of 175 days gives about 1.45 per year, and the non-KEV median of 621 days gives about 0.41 per year. Famous, publicized, internet-facing flaws are fixed about 10–20 times faster than typical known-exploited flaws.
- The residual is large and lasting. Heartbleed left 3.1% of Alexa sites vulnerable at 8 weeks, which is about 6–13% of the initially vulnerable sites (my calculation: 3.1/55 to 3.1/24). ProxyLogon left about 7.5% of on-premises servers at 3 weeks. Cyentia found 25% of all vulnerabilities open at one year. DBIR 2026 found 74% of KEVs not fully remediated within the period. Bitsight found 35% of organizations with at least one KEV visible from outside. A good functional form for "share of targets still open to an exposed tool" is r + (1 − r)·exp(−k·t), with k from about 1.5 per year (typical KEV) to 15–33 per year (famous flaw) and r from about 0.05 to 0.3. These ranges are my synthesis, not a fitted model.
- The scope is narrow. A patch fixes one flaw and often only the exploited path: at least half of the H1 2022 in-the-wild 0-days were avoidable with complete patches, and variants returned within 12 months. Indicators seen by one defender rarely reach others (fewer than 3% shared across feeds). The evidence therefore supports a specific part that acts per flaw or tool and per defender population, not a pooled quantity that lowers "every later attack's success ... whatever the target".
- Exposure hands the flaw to others. After disclosure, exploitation by other actors rises by orders of magnitude (older data), n-days are exploited within days to weeks, and edge KEVs are mass-exploited at day 0. The patch drive that ends the force's use of a flaw is thus partly driven by other actors' mass use, which the force does not control.
- Mapping to the model's inputs. **access_decay** (0.3 per year, range 0.1–1): RAND's 6.9-year mean life of a private zero-day gives about 0.145 per year if exponential; the RAND quartiles fit an exponential with that mean roughly (an exponential predicts a 25th percentile of about 2.0 years against 1.51, and a 75th percentile of about 9.6 years against 9.5; my calculation). This supports the lower half of the range for undisclosed access (older data; the dataset's source and years were not verified in this session). For exposed flaws, observed decay is 1.4–33 per year, far above the range: this contradicts one slow rate for both cases and supports an exposure-triggered jump. **burn_share** (0, range 0–0.8): exposure burns the specific flaw for 70–95% of targets within weeks to months, but the know-how survives (variants within 12 months; exploit development in a median of 22 days, older), which supports low values. **exposure**: see section 7.

## 2. General vigilance: does an exposed state attack make unrelated defenders more alert, and by how much does that lower the success of later, different attacks?

### Takeaway
I found no study that measures how much one exposed state attack lowers the success of later, different attacks on unrelated targets. The indirect evidence shows three things. First, the large gains in defense are slow and cumulative (dwell times of days instead of months; Ukraine had no critical incidents in the second half of 2025 while incident volume rose 37%). Second, exposed state actors resumed at full pace within about five months, and pre-positioned access survived years of rising alert. Third, under wartime alert in 2022, 29% of Russian intrusion attempts outside Ukraine still succeeded. A transient, pooled alertness that scales with the force's own exposures has little support.

### Inferences
- The strongest measured gains are slow capability trends (internal detection share rising from 43% to 52% in a year; MDR cases at 2 days of dwell against 5 days for incident-response cases; Ukraine's severity falling while volume rose). They track years of investment and outside help, not the number of exposures in the last year. The evidence favours a slow "structural hardening" stock with near-zero fade, separate from any fast attention term.
- Ukraine shows the specific–general split clearly. Industroyer2 reused the 2016 source code and was stopped before its effect; six months later the same actor used a new living-off-the-land method and caused an outage. This is consistent with strong specific adaptation (a known tool family) and weaker general vigilance against a new method, though the sources do not say why Industroyer2 was found.
- Exposure cost to the exposed actor is about 5 months of reduced activity (APT1, APT12; older), then full recovery with new infrastructure. Volt Typhoon rebuilt after a takedown. This bounds the attacker-side effect of exposure and does not show a lasting rise in defender success against the same actor.
- Size check against the model (my calculation). At a base success of 30%, the default alert_now of 0.05 log-odds lowers success to 29.0%, which no dataset could detect. Under the model's linear scaling, 22 times more exposures gives 1.1 log-odds and lowers success to 12.5%; alert_now = 0.1 gives 2.2 log-odds and about 4.5%; alert_now = 0.5 gives 11 log-odds and almost zero success. I found no evidence of cross-target drops of that size; Russian intrusion success stayed at 29% under wartime alert, and exploitation as an initial-access vector rose from 20% to 31% (DBIR 2026) while exposures multiplied.
- Defenders respond to the whole threat flow, not only to the force's exposures. In 2025 there were 884 new KEVs, 90 in-the-wild zero-days and 5,927 incidents at CERT-UA alone. If general alertness tracks the total public flow, a 22-fold rise in the force's own exposed attacks (from about 188 to about 4,100 a year at exposure 0.8) raises the total stimulus by much less than 22 times unless the force dominates the flow. This is an inference; the force's share of the flow is not measured.
- Mapping. **alert_now** (0.05, range 0–0.5): not measured anywhere; the default is not contradicted; values much above 0.1 under linear scaling imply large, unobserved drops at high attack rates. **alert_sensitivity** (0.3, range 0–1): espionage access persists through high alert (122-day median dwell for espionage, Volt Typhoon five years, edge devices with "inhibited detection"), which supports the default or lower values; not measured.

### Gaps

- No causal study of ISAC membership and breach rates was found.
- No estimate of cross-sector or cross-country spillover of vigilance was found beyond Ashraf's within-industry result (effect size not seen).

## 3. Saturation: does defender response grow linearly with the number of attacks, or does it saturate?

### Takeaway
Defender response saturates. Across organizations, remediation capacity is a fixed share of open work (about 1 in 10 open vulnerabilities closed per month, whatever the size). Over time, when the workload rose about 7.7 times (2022–2025), the share of KEVs fully fixed fell from 38% to 26% and the median time rose from 32 to 43 days. Between 40% and 67% of alerts are never investigated. Budgets and staff grow 4–7% a year. A fixed share of operators never responds to a specific warning. The evidence contradicts alertness that rises in proportion to the number of attacks.

### Cited Findings
- **Capacity proportional to backlog, not to threat**: "A typical organization, regardless of asset complexity, will have the capacity to remediate about one out of every 10 vulnerabilities in their environment within a given month"; "every tenfold increase in open vulnerabilities is met with a roughly tenfold increase in closed vulnerabilities"; "The R2 statistic for this log-log regression model is 0.93"; "Top Performers patch about 2.5 times more than others on average"; "Most firms cannot (or barely) keep up with the rate of exploited vulnerabilities added to their environment each month. But one-third manage to gain ground in that race." — [Cyentia/Kenna P2P Vol. 3, 2019](https://www.cyentia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kenna-Prioritization_to_Prediction-v3.pdf). Older. Read: primary-sections. Type: measurement (cross-section). Applies: transfer.
- **Workload outran improvement**: "The number of vulnerability instances in the dataset grew from 68.7 million in 2022 to 527 million in 2025"; "the volume grew faster than the improvement"; KEV full remediation fell to 26% from 38% and the median rose to 43 days. — [watchTowr on DBIR 2026, May 20, 2026](https://watchtowr.com/resources/verizon-dbir-2026-vulnerability-exploitation/). Read: secondary-fetched. Type: measurement. Applies: transfer. Part of the growth may be wider dataset coverage.
- **Deadlines missed**: "only 40% of KEVs are remediated by the deadline set by CISA", while the catalog grew by 17 a month in 2023. — [Bitsight, May 2024](https://www.bitsight.com/sites/default/files/2024-04/bitsight-a-global-view-of-cisa-kev-catalog.pdf). Read: primary-sections. Type: measurement. Applies: transfer.
- **Alert fatigue**: about 40% of security alerts are never investigated (Prophet Security survey of about 250–300 security leaders and practitioners, 2025) — [Prophet Security](https://www.prophetsecurity.ai/blog/ai-soc-statistics); Vectra surveys report 63–67% of alerts unaddressed — [Vectra](https://vectra.ai/topics/alert-fatigue). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement (vendor surveys; definitions differ). Applies: transfer.
- **Budgets and staff grow slowly**: security budgets grew 4% on average in 2025, down from 8% in 2024 (587 CISOs, April–August 2025); staffing growth slowed to 7%. — [IANS Research and Artico Search press release, 2025](https://www.iansresearch.com/resources/press-releases/detail/ians-research-and-artico-search-release-security-budget-benchmark-report). Only 47% of CISOs reported a budget increase, down from 62% in 2024 — [Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/101825-average-cyber-budget-reaches-5-year-low-cisos-discuss). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement (survey). Applies: transfer.
- **A ceiling on response to a specific warning**: notified and unnotified Heartbleed operators converged at "around 57% of contacts having reacted within a couple of weeks of notification". — [Durumeric et al. 2014](https://jhalderm.com/pub/papers/heartbleed-imc14.pdf). Older. Read: primary-sections. Type: measurement. Applies: transfer.
- **Prioritization under load**: CERT-UA's incident count rose 37.4% in 2025 while critical incidents fell to zero in the second half of the year. — [dev.ua](https://dev.ua/en/news/u-2025-rotsi-kilkist-kiberatak-zrosla-na-37-1767858414); [UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/no-critical-cyber-incidents-recorded-in-ukraine-by-the-end-of-2025-state-special-communications-service). Read: secondary-fetched. Type: measurement. Applies: direct.

### Inferences
- Across organizations, capacity scales with the size of the environment (organizations staff to their asset base), so the per-flaw closure rate is roughly constant (my calculation: 10% a month is about 1.26 per year). Over time, a surge in workload is not matched: budgets and staff grow by single-digit percentages, and completion shares fall. The general (attention) response should saturate; a form such as A = A_max·E/(E + E_half), or a logarithm of exposures, fits the evidence better than A ∝ E. The data do not fix A_max or E_half.
- The specific part can stay roughly linear per flaw (each exposed flaw gets its own patch at a roughly constant rate), but its rate falls when many flaws arrive at once, as in DBIR 2026.
- A large share of defenders does not respond to a specific warning, at least for weeks (about 43% of notified Heartbleed contacts had not reacted within about two weeks; end-of-life Ivanti appliances and embedded devices cannot be patched). This sets a floor on the residual r in section 1 that more alertness does not remove.
- With AI's "variety" (many targets, each hit in its own way), each exposure adds a new specific item to a saturated queue and little shared knowledge, so per-attack defender response falls as the attack count rises.

### Gaps
- No study measures defender response as a function of the number of state attacks, or any dose–response curve for general vigilance.
- No value for a saturation ceiling (A_max) or half-saturation point was found.
- The vendor alert-fatigue figures use different definitions and samples; no independent measurement was found.

## 4. Fade: how fast does each kind of adaptation decay?

### Takeaway
The three kinds of adaptation fade at very different speeds. Patches on fixed systems do not fade (no evidence of unpatching), but the unpatched tail lasts for years, vulnerable versions keep being re-introduced, and attackers return with variants within about 12 months. Human attention after training fades in about 4–6 months. An exposed actor recovers in about 5 months. Structural capability (detection speed, Ukraine's resilience) builds over years and shows no sign of fading. I found no quantitative data on attention cycles or on security-budget surges after major incidents.

### Cited Findings
- **No evidence of unpatching**: in Heartbleed scans, 6.5% of hosts went from immune to vulnerable between scans; the authors treat these as scanner false negatives, "Assuming that people do not unpatch their systems". — [Durumeric et al. 2014](https://jhalderm.com/pub/papers/heartbleed-imc14.pdf). Older. Read: primary-sections. Type: measurement plus author assumption. Applies: transfer.
- **Re-introduction and long tails**: 13% of 2025 Log4j downloads were still vulnerable four years after the fix — [Infosecurity Magazine, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/log4shell-downloaded-40-million/) (secondary-fetched); thousands of EternalBlue-vulnerable hosts five years after WannaCry — [SANS ISC](https://isc.sans.edu/diary/28816) (search-summary); 25% of vulnerabilities open after one year — [Cyentia P2P Vol. 3](https://www.cyentia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kenna-Prioritization_to_Prediction-v3.pdf) (older). Type: measurement. Applies: transfer.
- **Attackers return to patched bugs**: "Just 12 months from the original in-the-wild 0-day being patched, attackers came back with a variant of the original bug." — [Project Zero, June 30, 2022](https://projectzero.google/2022/06/2022-0-day-in-wild-exploitationso-far.html). Read: primary-full. Type: measurement. Applies: direct.
- **Human vigilance after training**: in 409 employees, phishing-detection performance was significantly better "directly after and four months after the programme's deployment"; "This was not the case anymore after six months, indicating that reminding users after half a year is recommended"; video and interactive reminders worked best, "lasting for at least another six months". — [Reinheimer et al., SOUPS 2020 (abstract)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer). Read: abstract. Type: measurement. Applies: analogy (individual staff, not organizations). A 2025 study of more than 1,300 employees reported that unsafe clicks and submissions fell by about half within six months under ongoing, mandatory training — [Help Net Security, Nov 7, 2025](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/11/07/research-phishing-training-effectiveness/). Read: search-summary.
- **Exposed actor's recovery**: APT1 delayed operations about 160 days and APT12 about 150 days before returning to pre-disclosure levels. — [The Register, April 11, 2014](https://www.theregister.com/2014/04/11/mandiant_china_apt_back_to_work/). Older. Read: secondary-fetched. Type: measurement. Applies: direct.
- **Budgets**: average security budget growth halved from 8% (2024) to 4% (2025), with no link to a specific incident in the source. — [IANS Research press release, 2025](https://www.iansresearch.com/resources/press-releases/detail/ians-research-and-artico-search-release-security-budget-benchmark-report). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement (survey). Applies: transfer.
- **Capability that persists**: internal detection rose to 52% in 2025; Ukraine had no critical incidents in H2 2025 after a decade of attacks; Iran's national build-up after Stuxnet lasted years. — [M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026); [UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/no-critical-cyber-incidents-recorded-in-ukraine-by-the-end-of-2025-state-special-communications-service); [Security Affairs, 2015](https://securityaffairs.com/35419/hacking/iran-cyber-capabilities.html). Type: measurement. Applies: direct for Ukraine and Iran.

### Inferences
- **Specific part**: the "fade" is not patch loss. It is the re-supply of vulnerable targets (new deployments of old versions, end-of-life devices) and of new paths to the same bug class. Variants within about 12 months suggest that the protection a single patch gives against a capable actor erodes at about 1 per year, while the patch itself holds. This is my inference from one Project Zero statement.
- **Fast general part (attention)**: training effects that vanish between 4 and 6 months suggest a decay rate of about 2–3 per year for human attention (my calculation, treating the threshold crossing as roughly one time constant; analogy only). The exposed actor's recovery (150–160 days) gives a similar rate on the attacker side, about 2.3 per year.
- **Slow general part (structure)**: decay well below 0.3 per year; the evidence shows accumulation, not decay.
- Mapping. **calm_rate** (1 per year, range 0.3–3): the range is supported. The evidence suggests two speeds rather than one: about 1–3 per year for attention, and near zero for structural hardening. A single pooled rate of 1 per year mixes the two.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of attention cycles after major incidents (search interest, media coverage, board attention) was found.
- No measurement of security-budget surges after named incidents, or of their fade, was found.
- No direct measurement of organizational patch backsliding was found.
- Phishing-training decay is an analogy for organizational vigilance, not a measurement of it.

## 5. How does AI change defender adaptation?

### Takeaway
AI speeds up finding and fixing flaws at the source. In DARPA's AI Cyber Challenge, systems patched in about 45 minutes at about $152 per task; Anthropic's Mythos Preview found thousands of high-severity flaws in every major operating system and browser; analysts with an AI assistant were 26% faster and 44% more accurate. AI also makes exploits for disclosed flaws cheap (vendor and secondary claims of about $1–3 and 10–15 minutes each) and pushes time-to-exploit below zero. Fixing across organizations, the real bottleneck, has not yet sped up: DBIR 2026 shows it slowed. Net effect: faster specific adaptation at vendors, cheaper replacement of burned tools, and so far no fleet-wide gain in patch speed.

### Cited Findings
- **DARPA AI Cyber Challenge final (DEF CON, August 2025)**: 63 synthetic vulnerabilities (corrected from 70); systems "discovered 54 unique" (about 86%) and "patched 43" (about 68%), up from 37% and 25% at the semifinals; 18 real vulnerabilities found and 11 real patches provided; "Teams submitted patches in an average of 45 minutes"; about $152 per competition task; over 54 million lines of code analysed. — [DARPA, Aug 8, 2025](https://darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results). Read: primary-full. Type: measurement (competition). Applies: transfer (open-source code, not deployed fleets). Earlier reports gave 77% and 61% using 70 as the denominator.
- **Project Glasswing (Anthropic, April 7, 2026)**: Claude Mythos Preview found "thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities", including "in every major operating system and every major web browser"; nearly all were found, and many exploits built, autonomously; the described examples "have all now been patched"; total patch counts and times are not stated; 11 named partners and more than 40 further organizations; CyberGym score 83.1% against 66.6% for Opus 4.6; Anthropic: AI models "can surpass all but the most skilled humans at finding and exploiting software vulnerabilities". A partner quote: "The window between a vulnerability being discovered and being exploited by an adversary has collapsed". An expanded Cyber Verification Program was announced on Oct 6, 2026. — [Anthropic, Project Glasswing page](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing). Read: primary-full. Type: measurement (counts) and opinion (quotes). Applies: direct (AI-enabled discovery at state-grade quality, put first in defenders' hands).
- **GTG-1002, an AI-run state espionage campaign that was exposed**: the attacker could "use AI to perform 80-90% of the campaign", with humans at "perhaps 4-6 critical decision points per hacking campaign"; "roughly thirty global targets"; success "in a small number of cases"; Claude "occasionally hallucinated credentials"; Anthropic detected the activity in mid-September 2025, investigated for about ten days while banning accounts, notified affected entities and coordinated with authorities. — [Anthropic, Nov 13, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage). Read: primary-full. Type: measurement. Applies: direct (exposure through the AI provider is a new exposure channel; it applies only when a force uses a commercial model).
- **Google CodeMender**: 72 security fixes upstreamed to open-source projects over about six months, each reviewed by humans. — [iTnews, Oct 2025](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/google-deepmind-tackles-software-vulnerabilities-with-ai-agent-620875). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement. Applies: transfer. No 2026 count for Google Big Sleep was found.
- **AI help for analysts**: in Microsoft's randomized trial, early-career analysts with Security Copilot were 26% faster and 44% more accurate — [Microsoft Security blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/?p=132571); an independent preprint RCT of 167 analysts using a phishing-triage agent reports up to 6.5 times as many true positives per analyst-minute and a 77% gain in verdict accuracy — [arXiv 2511.13860, Nov 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2511.13860). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement (vendor RCT; preprint). Applies: transfer.
- **AI makes exploits for disclosed flaws cheap**: researchers Efi Weiss and Nahman Khayet reported working exploits for published CVEs in about 10–15 minutes at about $1 each — [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/ai-generating-cves-in-just-10-15-minutes/amp/); [author's Substack](https://valmarelox.substack.com/p/can-ai-weaponize-new-cves-in-under); a vendor (Quantro Security) reported verified exploits for 2,183 of 3,029 disclosed CVEs (72%) at a median of $2.83 and 11 minutes — [Webull news](https://www.webull.com/news/15264714786628608). Read: search-summary. Type: measurement (unreviewed; vendor). Applies: transfer (mostly simple bugs).
- **Attacker speed**: average time-to-exploit estimated at -7 days in 2025 — [M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026); average eCrime breakout time 29 minutes in 2025 (fastest 27 seconds), which CrowdStrike credits to AI — [CrowdStrike press release, Feb 24, 2026](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/2026-crowdstrike-global-threat-report/) (search-summary); attackers reached Active Directory in a median of 3.4 hours, "sped up by 70% over last year" — [Sophos 2026](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/2026-sophos-active-adversary-report) (primary-full). Type: measurement (vendor). Applies: transfer.
- **Forecast**: "We anticipate that AI will accelerate the ongoing race between attackers and defenders in 2026"; "AI agents can proactively discover and help patch previously unknown security flaws". — [GTIG, March 5, 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/2025-zero-day-review). Read: primary-full. Type: estimate (forecast).
- **Deployment has not sped up**: KEV full remediation fell to 26% from 38%, and the median rose to 43 days. — [watchTowr on DBIR 2026](https://watchtowr.com/resources/verizon-dbir-2026-vulnerability-exploitation/). Read: secondary-fetched. Type: measurement. Applies: transfer.

### Inferences
- AI shortens the vendor half of specific adaptation (find and write the fix), not the deployment half (install the fix across organizations). The model's specific part should keep a deployment lag of weeks to months even when AI makes patches available in hours.
- If defenders' AI finds the same flaws first in widely used software, the life of a private zero-day (6.9 years in RAND's older data) should shorten, which raises **access_decay** for undisclosed access over 2026–2051. No measurement of this exists yet.
- Cheap exploit generation (minutes and dollars for disclosed flaws; RAND's older human median was 22 days) supports the essay's "AI makes a burned tool cheap to replace" and argues for a **burn_share** that is low and falls as AI's share of tactical work rises.
- AI's "variety" (each target hit in its own way) lowers the reach of specific adaptation, because each exposure teaches defenders about one tool used once. General gains from AI (analyst speed and accuracy, MDR detection in about 2 days) act on the slow, general part.
- Exposure through an AI provider (GTG-1002 was found and disrupted in about ten days) is a new channel that matters only when a force runs operations on a commercial model.

### Gaps
- No measured change in fleet-wide time to remediate that is due to AI.
- Glasswing patch counts and times to patch are not public in the pages I opened; a secondary report of more than 10,000 critical vulnerabilities in one month (May 2026) was seen only in a search result title and is not verified.
- No 2026 Big Sleep counts; no measurement of AI-generated signatures or detections after a tool exposure.
- The AI exploit-cost figures come from vendors and unreviewed write-ups.

## 6. Per stage: which numbers exist for each stage of the surveillance chain and the effects chain?

### Takeaway
Stage-level numbers exist mostly for criminal intrusions and for initial access. The surveillance and effects chains differ most in dwell: about 122 days median for espionage against 2–5 days for ransomware-type operations. Specific patching acts on initial access and on every link of an exploit chain; indicator sharing acts on infrastructure but reaches few defenders; general detection acts on lateral movement and exfiltration; and the effect stage was stopped only when defenders were inside the attacker's preparation window (Industroyer2 in 2022), not when the attacker brought a new method (October 2022).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A per-stage mapping that the evidence supports (my synthesis): initial access and each exploit-chain link → specific part (patch; fast phase plus residual, section 1); command-and-control infrastructure → specific part, local to the defenders who saw the indicators; lateral movement and exfiltration → general detection (dwell in days, improving slowly); effect → stopped mainly when the defender is already in the attacker's preparation window.
- A chain survives only if every link survives. With n links that each face a patch rate k, the chain's specific decay is about n·k, so a three-flaw mobile chain loses access about three times faster than one flaw. This is my inference.
- The surveillance chain's value depends on persistence, which survives long periods of alert (122 days median; years in pre-positioning); this supports a low **alert_sensitivity**. The effects chain is short (hours to days after access) and reveals itself, so **exposure** of effects attacks is high, with a delay from days to about a year before the tool is public.

### Gaps
- No per-stage success rates for state operations were found, except Microsoft's 29% intrusion success and the one-quarter exfiltration share for Russia in 2022.
- No per-stage decay rates of defender adaptation were found.
- Most stage timings come from criminal cases in vendor datasets; transfer to a state force's effects operations is uncertain.

## 7. What do the findings imply for each of the model's inputs?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports the ranges of calm_rate and exposure, supports a low burn_share and an alert_sensitivity at or below the default, and supports access_decay for undisclosed access but not for exposed access. It does not measure alert_now. It contradicts the structure of one pooled, linear alertness that lowers every later attack's success: the data point to a specific part per flaw or tool (fast, narrow, with a residual), a small and saturating attention part (decay about 1–3 per year), and a slow structural part (near-zero decay).

### Inferences
- **Specific part (per flaw or tool)**: on exposure, the share of targets still open to that tool falls as r + (1 − r)·exp(−k·t). Evidence-based bounds (my synthesis): k about 1.5 per year for a typical KEV, 15–33 per year for a famous internet-facing flaw; r about 0.05–0.3, with a floor set by non-responders (about 43% of notified Heartbleed contacts had not reacted within about two weeks; end-of-life devices cannot be patched). It applies only to later attacks that reuse the exposed flaw, tool or infrastructure. A patch erodes against a capable actor at about 1 per year through variants. Scope across defenders is narrow (fewer than 3% of indicators shared across feeds; 2019 data).
- **General attention part**: small, saturating in exposures, decay about 1–3 per year. Its size is not measured; the default 0.05 log-odds is consistent with the data, and saturation is better supported than linear growth.
- **Structural part**: slow accumulation from cumulative experience and investment (dwell time, internal detection share, Ukraine, browser hardening), near-zero decay, and probably the main channel by which "each exposure teaches defenders".
- With AI's variety and cheap replacement tools, the specific part reaches fewer later attacks, the attention part saturates, and the structural part grows slowly. Under these findings, more attacks raise defender adaptation much less than in proportion. Whether that makes doctrine easier or harder to form in the model depends on the correlation structure studied elsewhere; I state no result for the model.

### Gaps
- None of the six inputs has a direct measurement for a capable state's effects operations; all verdicts rest on transfer, analogy or older direct cases.
- No source gives a value for a saturation ceiling or for the force's share of the total exposure flow that defenders respond to.
- The share of the force's attacks that become public (exposure) is not measured anywhere I found.
